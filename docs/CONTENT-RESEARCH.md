# Content Research Guide

Before suggesting or writing an article, analyze the opportunity. This document
describes what to look for. **Nothing here guarantees rankings or traffic** — it is a
methodology for choosing topics with evidence of demand and reasonable room to compete.

Use it together with `docs/AI-PUBLISHING.md` and the `/content-ideas` dashboard.

## What to analyze for every topic

1. **Search trends** — Is demand flat, growing, or seasonal? Use Google Trends,
   Bing Webmaster keyword data, and YouTube search to gauge direction.
2. **Rising topics** — Are there related queries trending upward in the last 3–6 months?
   New product launches, OS updates, and software releases routinely create spikes.
3. **Search intent** — What does the searcher actually want? (Answer / how-to /
   troubleshooting / buy / compare). Match the article type to the intent:
   informational → explainer; procedural → how-to/tutorial; comparison → buying guide.
4. **Long-tail searches** — Real searches are long and specific
   ("how to clear junk files on windows 11 without third-party apps"). Long-tail pages
   are easier to rank and often convert better.
5. **Related questions** — Mine "People also ask", forums (Reddit, official support
   communities), and question sites. Each question is a potential section or FAQ entry.
6. **Competition** — For the target query, is the top content actually good? Weak,
   thin, or outdated top results are an opportunity; crowded pages with real depth are not.
7. **Content gaps** — Is the query answered at all? Is it answered only by
   low-quality AI spam or old listicles? A clearly better answer can win.
8. **Demand with weak content** — The best combination: evidence of demand (searches,
   forum posts, questions) + existing results that are thin or dated.

## Estimating opportunity honestly

- Use relative language: "steady demand", "rising interest", "competition is weak".
- Prefer qualitative signals you can point to (search volume ranges, forum activity,
  absence of good results) over invented numbers.
- Never guarantee a ranking, traffic level, or revenue.

## Future opportunities (6–36 months)

Build authority early in areas likely to grow:

- **AI** — practical AI tool evaluation, AI privacy and data controls, AI in daily tools.
- **Technology / software** — passkeys, AI OS features, subscription management.
- **Smartphones** — on-device AI, battery health, used-phone buying.
- **Gadgets** — Matter/Thread smart home, simple home setups.
- **Privacy & cybersecurity** — opt-outs, app tracking, account security.
- **Emerging digital services** — new authentication, data-portability, AI assistants.

When writing about a future topic, keep the content evergreen: explain the underlying
concept and the decision framework, not just the current product version.

## Where to look

- Google Trends (direction and seasonality)
- Bing Webmaster Tools (queries your site already appears for)
- Google "People also ask" and autocomplete
- Reddit / StackExchange / manufacturer support forums (real user problems and wording)
- Official documentation (authoritative, citable facts)
- Your own search data (which published articles get impressions but low clicks)

## Before writing

Write a one-line brief covering:

- **Primary keyword / query**
- **Related keywords**
- **Long-tail queries**
- **Search intent**
- **Related questions**
- **Suggested tags**
- **Suggested internal links** (to existing articles/categories)
- **Image plan** (subject, source type, alt text)

Then follow the publish workflow: Research → AI Draft → Humanize → Fact Check →
SEO → Human Review → Publish.
