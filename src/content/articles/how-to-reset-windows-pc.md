Resetting a Windows PC is the computer equivalent of moving to a new house: everything feels fresh, but you want to be sure you packed what you needed first. Windows makes the process genuinely simple — two options on one screen — but choosing wrong, or skipping the backup, is where people get hurt.

Here's exactly what a reset does, what each option keeps and deletes, and how to do it without losing anything that matters.

## What "Reset this PC" actually does

Windows 11's reset reinstalls a clean copy of Windows. It's faster than a full reinstall, needs no installation media, and handles drivers and activation for you. It comes in two flavors, and the difference is exactly what it sounds like:

- **Keep my files** — reinstalls Windows, keeps your personal files, removes apps and settings.
- **Remove everything** — reinstalls Windows, wipes all files, apps, users, and settings. The full factory reset.

The biggest surprise for most people is the middle ground. "Keep my files" sounds like it keeps everything, but it doesn't keep your **apps**. Every program you installed gets removed — you'll need to reinstall them. Only your personal files (Documents, Pictures, Music, Desktop items, and browser favorites) stay.

## Back up first. No exceptions.

A reset should be a reset, not a tragedy. Before you do anything:

- **Cloud backup:** Move anything irreplaceable to cloud storage (OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud) or email it to yourself. Photos and documents first.
- **External drive:** Copy your most important folders to a USB drive. For the truly cautious, a full image backup is the gold standard — Windows' own **Backup** settings can create one.
- **Browser data:** If you use a browser without sync, export your bookmarks before resetting.
- **Software licenses:** Note down any product keys or license details for apps you'll need to reinstall.

If you back up to an external drive, unplug it before you start the reset. Windows won't touch it, but "Remove everything" is thorough and you don't want a stray click erasing your only copy.

Your phone probably needs the same treatment — our [guide to backing up an iPhone](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) covers the mobile side while you're thinking about it.

## Before you reset: quick checks

Two things to settle first:

1. **Do you need a reset at all?** A reset is a big hammer. If your PC is just slow, try the [speed-up guide for Windows 11](/articles/speed-up-windows-11-pc) first — most slowness is fixable without a wipe.
2. **Can you sign in after?** A reset that removes everything will ask for your Microsoft account or local account password afterward. Make sure you know it before you start.

## How to reset: step by step

The steps are identical whether you're keeping or removing files — the difference is one click.

1. Open **Settings → System → Recovery**.
2. Under **Reset this PC**, click **Reset PC**.
3. Choose **Keep my files** or **Remove everything** (see the comparison above).
4. If given the option, choose **Cloud download** or **Local reinstall**. Cloud download fetches a fresh copy from Microsoft's servers (needs an internet connection, around a few GB); local reinstall uses the copy already on your PC. Cloud is the more thorough of the two.
5. If asked, choose **Change settings** to review what the reset will remove — this screen spells out exactly what's going.
6. Click **Next**, then **Reset**, and let it run.

A note on the wording: "Remove everything" also gives you a choice about cleaning the drive. **Just remove my files** is enough for a normal reset. The **fully clean the drive** option takes much longer and is only really needed if you're selling or donating the PC.

## How long does it take?

Plan for somewhere between 20 minutes and an hour, with these rules of thumb:

- **Keep my files** is faster than **Remove everything**.
- **Local reinstall** is faster than **Cloud download**.
- An SSD makes it dramatically faster than an old hard drive.

Plug the PC into power, and don't interrupt it — the screen may sit on a percentage for a while, and that's normal. Walk away and let it finish.

## What "cloud download" vs "local reinstall" means

Windows may ask how it should get the fresh copy of Windows it needs — and the choice matters.

- **Cloud download** pulls a current copy from Microsoft's servers. It needs a working internet connection and a few gigabytes of data, but you get the latest version of Windows. That's a real advantage when the local copy on your PC is itself damaged — which is often exactly why you're resetting.
- **Local reinstall** uses the Windows files already on your drive. It's faster and works offline, but if the problem is corrupted system files, some of that corruption can carry over into the fresh install.

If your internet connection is fine, cloud download is usually the safer choice for a PC that's been misbehaving.

## What you'll need to redo afterward

Set expectations now so there are no surprises when you log back in:

- **Every app** needs reinstalling (the big time cost — budget an afternoon if you have a lot).
- **Settings** go back to defaults: Wi-Fi passwords, wallpaper, display settings, and app preferences.
- **OneDrive and browser sync** will pull your files and favorites back automatically if you're signed in.
- **Peripherals** mostly work out of the box, but printer and scanner software usually needs reinstalling.

In exchange, you get a Windows installation that behaves like day one — no leftover junk, no mystery slowdowns, no years of accumulated weirdness.

## Wrapping up

Choose "Keep my files" if your personal files are the priority and you're fine reinstalling apps. Choose "Remove everything" only for a true clean slate or before handing the PC on. Back up first no matter what, plug in the power, and don't interrupt once it starts. The result is a PC that feels new again.
