Taking a screenshot on a Mac is easy — the entire system is built in, no extra software needed. You just have to know the three shortcuts, and they follow a simple pattern once you see it.

The quick version: **Cmd+Shift+3** captures the whole screen, **Cmd+Shift+4** lets you drag over a specific area, and **Cmd+Shift+5** opens a toolbar with every option including screen recording. Here's how each one works, and where your captures go.

## The three main shortcuts

### 1. Capture the whole screen: Cmd+Shift+3

Press Cmd+Shift+3 and your entire display is captured instantly. If you have multiple monitors, each one gets its own screenshot file. You'll hear a shutter sound, and the image appears on your Desktop.

### 2. Capture a selected area: Cmd+Shift+4

This is the one you'll use most. Press Cmd+Shift+4 and the cursor becomes a crosshair. Drag over the part of the screen you want, then release.

While dragging, a few modifiers help:

- Hold **Shift** to lock the selection's height or width as you drag.
- Hold **Option** to resize the selection from its center instead of a corner.
- Press and hold **spacebar** while dragging to move the whole selection box around.
- Press **Esc** at any point to cancel without taking a shot.

### 3. Open the screenshot toolbar: Cmd+Shift+5

Cmd+Shift+5 brings up a small floating toolbar at the bottom of the screen. It gives you the same whole-screen and selection options, plus a few extra: capturing a single window, and recording your screen as video (with or without audio).

The toolbar is also where you adjust settings, so it's worth opening it once to set things up the way you like.

## How to capture a single window

From the Cmd+Shift+4 shortcut, press the **spacebar**. The crosshair changes into a small camera icon. Hover over any window — it highlights as you pass over it — and click to capture just that window, complete with its drop shadow.

You can do the same from the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar by clicking the window-capture button.

## Where your screenshots go

By default, every screenshot is saved as a `.png` file on your **Desktop**, named with the date and time — something like `Screenshot 2026-08-23 at 3.14.25 PM.png`.

That's the answer to the most common "my screenshot disappeared" panic: check the Desktop first. If you don't want them cluttering your desktop, you can redirect them anywhere.

### How to change the save location

1. Press **Cmd+Shift+5** to open the toolbar.
2. Click **Options**.
3. Under **Save to**, choose a new destination — Documents, Downloads, or a folder you made called Screenshots.
4. Take a screenshot and confirm it appears in the new spot.

## The clipboard variants

Sometimes you don't want a file at all — you want the capture pasted straight into a document, email, or chat. Add **Ctrl** to the mix:

- **Cmd+Ctrl+Shift+3** — copy the whole screen to your clipboard.
- **Cmd+Ctrl+Shift+4** — capture a selection to your clipboard.
- **Cmd+Ctrl+Shift+5** — the toolbar variant.

After copying, just paste with Cmd+V wherever you need it. No file is saved to your Desktop.

## Editing screenshots without extra apps

Every screenshot you take can be edited with tools already on your Mac, so there's no need to install anything for the common touch-ups.

1. Double-click the screenshot on your Desktop to open it in Preview.
2. Click the **markup** icon (the pencil tip) in the toolbar to reveal the drawing tools.
3. Use the arrow, shape, and text tools to annotate, or the selection tool to crop. Drag over an area and press Cmd+K to crop.

Preview also lets you add a signature, highlight parts of an image, or send it to someone directly from the Share menu. It's quick, and it handles 90% of "I need to circle something and send it" situations.

If you find yourself screenshotting constantly, you can make your life easier: set the save location to a dedicated Screenshots folder (see below), and drag that folder into the Finder sidebar so it's one click away.

## Touch Bar and special cases

If your Mac has a Touch Bar, you can capture it with **Cmd+Shift+6**.

For a screenshot that includes the mouse cursor (or hides it), open the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar, click Options, and pick Show Mouse Pointer. And if you're using a MacBook, the screenshot shortcuts work with the built-in keyboard — no function-key gymnastics needed.

## Troubleshooting the common snags

- **Screenshot saved to Desktop not showing.** You may have redirected the save location earlier. Open the Cmd+Shift+5 toolbar and check Options → Save to.
- **Shutter sound plays but no file.** Check the clipboard — you might have hit a Ctrl variant. Paste with Cmd+V to confirm.
- **Shortcut does nothing.** Screenshot shortcuts can be blocked by other apps or by a different keyboard layout. Go to System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Screenshots to see and reset them.
- **You want a file, not a clipboard copy.** Use the plain shortcuts without Ctrl.

If you're moving to a Mac from a PC, this is one of the few places where Windows is actually *different* — on Windows it's Print Screen or Win+Shift+S. On a Mac, it's the Cmd+Shift trio above. Both are quick once you're used to them.

## Wrapping up

There's no need for third-party screenshot apps on a Mac. Cmd+Shift+3 for the full screen, Cmd+Shift+4 to drag a selection, Cmd+Shift+5 for the toolbar and screen recording — and add Ctrl to any of them to send the capture to your clipboard instead of your Desktop.
