Moving to a new Android phone should feel exciting, not stressful. The good news: almost everything you care about can move with you, and you don't need a paid app or a computer to do it. The tools are built into every Android phone.

Here's the honest overview before the steps. Your Google account carries a surprising amount — contacts, calendar, email, and backed-up photos follow your sign-in to any Android phone. The setup assistant that runs when you first turn on a new phone handles the rest: apps, messages, and most settings. The one thing that often needs its own step is chat history in apps like WhatsApp.

## What transfers automatically with your Google account

Anything tied to your Google account is already "backed up" in the practical sense — sign into that account on the new phone and it comes with you.

- **Contacts and calendar** — synced automatically when you add your Google account.
- **Email** — it lives on Google's servers, so it's already there.
- **Photos and videos** — if you have backup enabled in Google Photos, they're in the cloud and appear on the new phone when you sign in.
- **App data for many apps** — apps that use Google Drive backup (check each app's settings) restore their data during setup.

So step one of any move: know your Google account password and make sure it's working on the old phone before you switch.

## Method 1: Use the built-in setup assistant (easiest)

The fastest way to move everything is to do it while the new phone is still in its box. Android's setup assistant asks, early in the process, whether you want to copy apps and data from an old device. This works when moving from another Android phone, and it can also pull data from an iPhone.

1. Turn on the new phone and start setup, connecting to Wi-Fi when prompted.
2. When asked how to copy your data, choose **"Copy apps and data from an old device"** (or similar wording).
3. Pick **"From an Android phone"**, then connect the two phones — with a cable if you have one, or by scanning a QR code shown on the new phone.
4. Choose what to copy: contacts, messages, photos, apps, call history, and settings.
5. Let it run. Large photo libraries and app lists can take a while, so keep both phones plugged in and leave them alone.

A few honest notes. The assistant copies what it can, but it does not copy everything: you'll still sign into some apps again, and chat apps like WhatsApp have their own restore process. Also, if the cable connection isn't recognized, the wireless option works fine — it's just slower.

## Method 2: Google account sync (when the new phone is already set up)

Missed the setup assistant window, or already handed over the old phone? No problem. Signing into your Google account on the new phone brings most of your data back with it.

1. On the new phone, open **Settings → Accounts → Add account → Google** and sign in.
2. Once signed in, open the account settings and make sure **sync** is on for Contacts, Calendar, and anything else you want.
3. Wait for the first sync to finish — contacts and calendar usually appear within minutes.
4. Install your apps from the Google Play Store. Apps you'd downloaded before are listed under your account in the Play Store if you want a reminder of what you had.

This is also the method to use after a factory reset, when you're setting the same phone up fresh.

## Method 3: Move photos and videos your way

Photos are the heaviest part of any move, so they deserve their own section.

- **If Google Photos backup is on:** sign in on the new phone and your photos appear in the app automatically. Nothing to copy.
- **If it's not on:** turn on **Backup** in the Google Photos app on the old phone and let it upload before you switch. Large libraries take time, so start this a day or two ahead if you can.
- **Prefer no cloud?** Connect the old phone to a computer with a USB cable, copy the DCIM folder (where camera photos live) to the computer, then copy it to the new phone the same way. A cloud drive works too.

This is the step that trips people up the most, and it's also the one where our [guide to freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) becomes useful — moving photos off your old phone clears space there, and you can use the same tricks to keep the new one tidy.

## What about WhatsApp and other chat apps?

This is the one part of a phone move that isn't automatic, and it's worth saying plainly: chat history does not move with a standard transfer. Each chat app keeps its own records, and most use their own backup system.

For WhatsApp specifically:

1. On the old phone, open WhatsApp → **Settings → Chats → Chat backup** and back up to your Google account.
2. On the new phone, install WhatsApp and verify your number.
3. When the app detects a backup, follow the prompt to **restore** it.

Other apps — Telegram, Signal, and the like — each have their own storage or backup settings. Check the app's own settings before you wipe the old phone, because once it's gone, the history is usually gone.

## Before you let go of the old phone

Take a breath and verify before you reset or sell anything.

- Confirm the new phone has your contacts, at least some photos, and the apps you use daily.
- Keep the old phone for a few days as a safety net. There's no rush.
- If you're handing the old phone to someone else, do a full factory reset (Settings → System → Reset options) so none of your data goes with it.

## Wrapping up

The order matters more than the tools: back up photos first (cloud upload takes the longest), use the setup assistant for the bulk of the move, sign into your Google account on the new phone, and handle WhatsApp before you wipe anything. Do it in that order and you'll be up and running on the new phone the same day, with almost nothing left behind.
