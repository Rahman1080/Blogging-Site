RAM is short-term memory. When you open an app or a browser tab, the device loads what it needs into RAM so the processor can work with it instantly. When RAM runs out, the device starts shuffling data to slower storage — and that's when things get sluggish.

So how much do you actually need? The honest answer: it depends on what you do, and a lot of people are buying either too little or too much. Here are practical starting points, not rigid rules.

## How much RAM for a laptop or desktop

### Basic browsing, email, and office work

If your day is web tabs, documents, spreadsheets, and video calls, **16 GB is the comfortable default in 2026**. It handles dozens of tabs plus a video call without thinking. 8 GB still works for very light use, but it's the minimum, not a good target — and many machines now have RAM you can't upgrade later.

### Creative work and heavier apps

Photo editing, audio work, design tools, and light video editing start to breathe at **16 GB** and get comfortable at **32 GB**. If your work involves 3D, virtual machines, or long video timelines, more RAM directly means fewer waits.

### Gaming

**16 GB is the sweet spot for gaming** today. 32 GB is a luxury that helps in a few heavy titles and long sessions with background apps open. 8 GB is going to hurt in modern games.

### Is 8 GB of RAM enough in 2026?

For a phone, often yes. For a computer, only for genuinely light use — and only if the machine lets you upgrade later, because it's tight. If you buy a laptop for several years of everyday work, 16 GB is the safer call. Our [how to choose a laptop guide](/articles/how-to-choose-a-laptop-buying-guide) treats RAM as one of the few specs that decide whether a laptop stays fast.

## How much RAM for a phone

Phones use RAM the same way, but they're managed differently, so the numbers don't compare directly to computers.

- **iPhone:** Apple is famously efficient with memory. An iPhone with less RAM than an Android rival can still run smoother because the software and hardware are designed together. For most iPhone owners, the RAM question basically doesn't come up — you buy by storage and model, not RAM.
- **Android:** more RAM genuinely helps, because Android keeps more apps running in the background, and it's a common spec tier (you'll see phones with 6, 8, 12, or 16 GB). If you're a heavy multitasker or you keep lots of apps open, the higher tiers are worth considering.

For everyday use on either platform, RAM is rarely the reason a phone feels slow — a tired battery or a full storage drive is more often the culprit. Our guides to [freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) and [speeding up a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) cover the fixes that actually help.

## 8 GB vs 16 GB: the practical difference

If you're weighing 8 GB against 16 GB, here's the realistic difference:

- With 8 GB, you'll notice slowdowns when lots is open — many tabs, a big spreadsheet, an app that grew since you bought the machine.
- With 16 GB, you basically stop thinking about RAM. It's the "set and forget" option.

## Signs you need more RAM

If you're wondering whether your current machine is short on RAM, look for these:

- Your device slows down when many tabs or apps are open, then speeds up when you close them.
- Switching between apps takes a beat.
- You see memory warnings in the operating system or in apps.
- Fan noise and sluggishness spike during normal multitasking.

If those sound familiar and you can't add RAM, closing apps and tabs you're not using helps — but the long-term fix is buying more next time.

## How to check how much RAM you have

1. **Windows:** press **Ctrl + Shift + Esc** to open Task Manager, then the **Performance** tab and the **Memory** section.
2. **Mac:** click the Apple menu → **About This Mac**, and look at the **Memory** line.
3. **Android:** open **Settings** → **About phone**, and look for the RAM or memory entry (the location varies by brand).
4. **iPhone:** Apple doesn't list RAM in the Settings app — check the model specs on Apple's site instead.

## RAM vs storage: the confusion killer

People mix these up all the time, and it's worth clearing up because it changes what you buy. RAM is temporary working memory — it holds what's open right now and forgets it when you restart. Storage (the hard drive or SSD) is the permanent home for your files and apps.

A phone "with 256 GB" is telling you about storage, not RAM. A laptop "with 16 GB" is telling you about RAM. And the two cause different problems when they run low: storage fills up and things stop saving; RAM runs out and everything stutters. Our guides to [freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) and [speeding up a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) hit both angles for phones.

## Wrapping up

Think of RAM as elbow room: too little and everything feels cramped, too much and you paid for space you never use. For a computer, 16 GB covers the vast majority of people; phones are simpler — buy by storage and model, not the RAM sticker. Match RAM to your heaviest habit and you'll be set for years.
