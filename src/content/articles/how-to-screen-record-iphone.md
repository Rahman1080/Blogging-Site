Recording your iPhone screen is a built-in feature — there's no app to buy, nothing to install, and it takes about thirty seconds to set up. It's useful for capturing a bug to show someone, making a quick tutorial, or saving a moment in an app that won't let you download it.

Here's how to record your screen, with or without sound, and how to manage the results.

## Step 1: Add Screen Recording to Control Center

The recording button lives in Control Center — the panel you swipe down from. On most iPhones it's already there, but if you don't see it, here's how to add it:

1. Open **Settings → Control Center**.
2. Find **Screen Recording** in the list.
3. Tap the **plus button** next to it to add it to the "Included Controls" section.
4. Optional: press and hold the **grip icon** and drag it to wherever you like in the Control Center.

Now the button is one swipe away whenever you need it.

## Step 2: Record the screen (no sound)

This is the fastest path — good for when you don't need your voice.

1. **Swipe down** from the top-right corner of the screen (or swipe up from the bottom on older iPhones with a Home button) to open Control Center.
2. Tap the **Screen Recording** button — a circle inside a ring.
3. A three-second countdown starts, then the recording begins. The button turns red, and a **red indicator** appears at the top of the screen.

That's it — everything on your screen from that moment is being captured.

## Step 3: Record with sound (your voice, or any audio)

To capture audio, you need to turn the microphone on first. This is the step everyone misses.

1. Open Control Center.
2. **Press and hold** the Screen Recording button — don't just tap it. A small menu appears.
3. Tap the **microphone icon** at the bottom so it turns red (on).
4. Tap **Start Recording**.

With the mic on, your voice and the sounds around you are captured. One honest note: this records *your* microphone, not the app's internal audio. Music and video apps usually block their own audio from being captured, so the result is often your voice with no background music — that's an app restriction, not something you're doing wrong. Some apps handle their own internal audio in recordings, and if you're recording a game, the game's sound frequently comes through alongside the mic.

## Step 4: Stop the recording

Stop it any time, in either of two ways:

- Tap the **red status bar** at the top of the screen and confirm **Stop**.
- Open Control Center and tap the Screen Recording button, which is now red.

The recording automatically saves to **Photos** and is ready to share.

## Step 5: Find and manage your recordings

Recordings land in the **Photos** app, exactly like videos:

- Open **Photos** and look in **Albums → Recents**. They're usually named like `RPReplay_2026-08-23_...`.
- Tap one to watch it.

### Trim a recording

Apple's built-in editor handles the basics without any extra app:

1. In Photos, open the recording and tap **Edit**.
2. Drag the yellow trim handles at the bottom to cut off the beginning or end.
3. Tap **Done** and choose whether to save a new clip or replace the original.

### Share or save it

Tap the **Share** button (square with an up arrow) to send it anywhere — Messages, Mail, AirDrop, or a cloud drive. If you want it off the phone entirely, our guide on [transferring photos from iPhone to a computer](/articles/how-to-transfer-photos-iphone-computer) applies to recordings too.

## Common mistakes to avoid

These trip up most first-time recorders:

- **Forgetting the mic is off by default.** Press and hold the button to switch it on — a quick tap records silently every time.
- **Locking the phone.** If the screen locks mid-recording, you'll capture a lock screen, not whatever you wanted. Adjust Auto-Lock in Settings first.
- **Not checking where recordings go.** They save to Photos, not to a "recordings" folder. If you can't find one, look in Photos → Albums → Recents.
- **Forgetting you're live.** The red indicator at the top is the only sign a recording is running — and it doesn't appear in your own video. If you don't want the world to see you fiddling with settings, stop the recording before you open anything sensitive.
- **Letting long recordings eat all your space.** A screen recording is a video, and videos are storage hogs. For anything long, plan the space beforehand or accept the cleanup task after.

## Extra tips worth knowing

- **The countdown is skippable in effect** — tap the red indicator as soon as the recording starts if you changed your mind, and you can stop it right away.
- **Don't want to miss a recording?** Keep an eye on the red status bar — it's the indicator that a recording is live. The phone still works normally while recording; notifications and other apps behave as usual.
- **Recordings respect the screen**: if you lock the phone, the recording continues but captures the lock screen. If you need to record something that requires staying awake, adjust your auto-lock: **Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock** and set it longer, or never. ## Wrapping up

Add the button to Control Center once, tap it to record silently, or press and hold and enable the mic to capture your voice. Stop it from the red status bar, and your recording lands in Photos, ready to trim or share. No extra app, no setup, no cost.
