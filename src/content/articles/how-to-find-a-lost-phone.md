# How to Find a Lost Phone (Android and iPhone)

The moment you realize your phone is gone is a small panic. The good news: if you set up the free tracking feature before this happened — Find My on iPhone, Find My Device on Android — you can almost certainly see exactly where it is from any computer within minutes.

If you didn't set it up in advance, there's still hope, but your options shrink. So let's cover the ideal case first, then what to do when tracking isn't working or the phone is gone for good.

## Set expectations before you panic

Tracking only works if the phone had the feature enabled and was connected to the internet. That's why the setup step matters more than the recovery step. If your phone is lost right now and the feature was on, great. If it was off, skip straight to "when you can't find it" below.

Both services also show the phone's last known location even if it's currently offline, and newer phones can often report a location for a while after being powered off — but don't rely on live tracking once the battery dies.

## Find a lost iPhone

Apple's tracking tool is called **Find My**, and you can use it from any browser without downloading anything.

1. Go to **iCloud.com/find** on any computer or another device and sign in with your Apple ID.
2. The Find My map opens automatically. If you have multiple devices, pick your iPhone from the device list.
3. The phone's location appears as a dot on the map. You'll see options beneath it:
   - **Play Sound** — makes the phone ring loudly even if it's on silent. Ideal for a phone that's stuck between couch cushions.
   - **Directions** — gets walking or driving directions to the location.
   - **Mark as Lost** — locks the phone, displays a message with a contact number on the lock screen, and disables Apple Pay.
   - **Erase** — wipes the phone remotely. Only do this if you've given up on getting it back, because it removes the tracking too.

**A quick tip:** if you use a family group, any family member's iPhone can open the **Find My app**, tap Devices, and locate the missing phone. Ask a relative if you don't have another Apple device handy.

## Find a lost Android phone

Google's equivalent is **Find My Device**, and it works the same way from a browser.

1. Go to **google.com/android/find** (or use the Find My Device website) and sign in with the Google account that was on the phone.
2. If you have multiple devices, select the missing phone.
3. The map shows its location, with these options:
   - **Play sound** — rings the phone at full volume for a few minutes, even on silent.
   - **Secure device** — locks the phone and signs you out of your Google account on it.
   - **Erase device** — wipes the data. Use this only if you've accepted the phone is gone, because it also disables the ability to find it afterward.

Android's Find My Device requires the phone to be on, signed into your Google account, and connected to the internet — the same caveat as iPhone. Some newer Android phones support locating the device briefly after it's powered off, but it's not guaranteed across brands.

## What to do when you can't find it

Sometimes the phone is off, out of coverage, or simply never had tracking enabled. When the map won't help, switch to damage control:

1. **Change your important passwords** — email, banking, and social media first. Do this from a computer.
2. **Lock the phone remotely if you can** — even without a location, both services let you sign out accounts and lock the screen.
3. **Call your carrier** — they can suspend the line or the SIM so nobody runs up charges on your number.
4. **Report it to the police** if you believe it was stolen — some insurers and carriers require a police report.
5. **Restore your data on a new phone** — this is where a good backup pays for itself. Our guide to [backing up an iPhone](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) covers keeping your data safe, and the same principle applies on Android with Google's automatic backups.

## The prevention habit that actually helps

If you've just recovered your phone or bought a new one, spend two minutes confirming tracking is on:

- **iPhone:** **Settings → your name → Find My → Find My iPhone** — make sure it's on, and consider enabling **Send Last Location**, which automatically sends the phone's location to Apple when the battery is critically low.
- **Android:** **Settings → Security → Find My Device** (the path varies by brand) — make sure it's on. Some phones also have a "location before power off" option worth enabling.

That two-minute check is the single thing that separates "I know exactly where my phone is" from "I have no idea." It's also worth remembering that a phone full of old data and apps is harder to track meaningfully anyway — cleaning it up periodically is good hygiene, and freeing storage keeps things running smoothly. See our guide to [freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) for that.

## Wrapping up

Finding a lost phone comes down to one setup step and one recovery step. Turn on Find My or Find My Device now, while you still have the phone. If it goes missing later, sign in from a browser, look at the map, and use Play Sound before you escalate to lock or erase. And if the worst happens, your backup is the real safety net.
