Putting your laptop on the TV is one of the most useful tech tricks there is — movies on the big screen, presentations, family photos, or a browser you don't have to squint at. And it's simpler than it looks, because there are really just two paths: a cable, or a wireless connection.

The short version: **HDMI** is the reliable route that works with basically every laptop and TV, and **wireless casting** is the no-cable option if both devices support it. Here's how to do both, plus the fixes for the inevitable "it's not showing up" moment.

## The wired route: HDMI (most reliable)

HDMI is the universal standard for connecting a laptop to a TV. Practically every TV made in the last decade has at least one HDMI port, and nearly every laptop has HDMI — or a USB-C port that can drive one.

### What you'll need

- An **HDMI cable** (any standard one works).
- A **USB-C to HDMI cable or adapter** if your laptop only has USB-C ports. Note: not every USB-C port can output video. If you have more than one, try them all — one is usually the display-capable one.

### Step-by-step

1. **Connect the cable** from your laptop to any HDMI port on the TV.
2. **Turn on the TV** and switch it to the matching input. Look for the Input or Source button on the remote and select the HDMI port you plugged into (HDMI 1, HDMI 2, and so on).
3. **Wait a few seconds.** The laptop screen should appear on the TV, either mirrored or extended, automatically.
4. **If nothing happens**, tell the laptop there's a display there:
   - **Windows:** press **Win+P** and choose **Duplicate** (same image on both) or **Extend** (two separate screens).
   - **Mac:** open **System Settings → Displays** and click **Detect Displays**.

That's it. The cable route has no software quirks, no lag, and works with any TV — it's the option to pick when the wireless stuff refuses to cooperate.

## The wireless route: no cable needed

Wireless is convenient, but it depends on what your TV supports. Two standards cover most setups:

### Windows: Cast

If you have a Windows laptop and a smart TV (or a streaming device like a Chromecast or an Xbox), you can use the built-in **Cast** feature:

1. Make sure the laptop and TV are on the **same Wi-Fi network**.
2. Press **Win+K** to open the Cast panel.
3. Select your TV or streaming device from the list.
4. Choose how you want to share — often the option is **Duplicate** or **Extend**.

### Mac: AirPlay

If you have a Mac and an **Apple TV**, or a TV that advertises **AirPlay** support, use AirPlay:

1. Make sure both are on the same Wi-Fi network.
2. Click the **Control Center** icon in the menu bar (top-right).
3. Click **Screen Mirroring**.
4. Select your Apple TV or AirPlay-compatible TV.

Wireless is great for movies and presentations, but be honest about its limits: there's a small delay between what happens on the laptop and what appears on the TV. For gaming or anything where timing matters, use HDMI.

## Quick troubleshooting: nothing shows up

This is the most common stumbling block, and it's usually one of these four things:

- **Wrong input selected.** The TV is showing its home screen or "No Signal" because you're on the wrong HDMI input. Press Input/Source on the remote and cycle through until the laptop appears.
- **Cable not fully seated.** HDMI plugs are snug; push both ends in until they click. If you're using an adapter, check both ends of the adapter too.
- **The laptop doesn't know a display is attached.** On Windows, Win+P then Duplicate/Extend. On Mac, System Settings → Displays → Detect Displays. Sometimes re-plugging the cable once also wakes it up.
- **Wireless device not showing in the list.** Both devices must be on the same network, and some TVs need a setting enabled (often under Network or "Screen Mirroring" in the TV's menu). If it still won't appear, restart the TV and laptop — that fixes a surprising share of wireless pairing issues.

If you're still stuck, check whether the Wi-Fi itself is the problem — a flaky connection can make wireless casting drop out. Our [Wi-Fi not working guide](/articles/wifi-not-working-fixes) has the fixes for that.

## Getting the sound right

When the picture works but there's no sound, the audio is still going to the laptop's speakers. That's easy to fix:

- **Windows:** click the speaker icon in the taskbar, click the device name above the volume slider, and select the TV (it usually shows the brand or "Digital Output").
- **Mac:** open **System Settings → Sound → Output** and select the TV.

If the sound comes out of the TV *and* the laptop, or sounds out of sync, check the same settings — the fix is picking the TV as the single output.

One more common surprise: older TVs only play audio from certain HDMI inputs, and some need the "HDMI ARC" port if you want audio to return from the TV back to another device. For plain laptop-to-TV video, any HDMI port works.

## When it still won't work

A few edge cases worth knowing before you call it a hardware fault:

- **The TV doesn't have an HDMI port.** Very old TVs use VGA or composite inputs. A simple adapter (HDMI to VGA, for example) handles this, though sound needs a separate cable.
- **The wireless option isn't on your TV's menu.** Look for names like "Screen Mirroring," "Miracast," or "AirPlay" in the TV's settings. If none exist, the TV likely can't receive wireless casting, and HDMI is your only path.
- **Resolution looks stretched or tiny.** Windows: right-click the desktop → Display settings → pick a resolution that matches the TV. Mac: System Settings → Displays → choose the resolution under the TV's name.

## A note on choosing laptops

If you're shopping and display output matters to you, look for a laptop with an HDMI port or a USB-C port that supports video out. Not all budget laptops include HDMI, and the adapter route is mildly annoying. The [laptop buying guide](/articles/how-to-choose-a-laptop-buying-guide) covers ports and everything else worth checking before you buy.

## Wrapping up

Plug in an HDMI cable and pick the right input for a connection that always works. Use Cast (Windows) or AirPlay (Mac) when you want no cables and both devices support it. And when nothing appears, it's almost always the input, the cable, or a laptop that hasn't noticed the display yet.
