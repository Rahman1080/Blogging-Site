Buying a used phone can save you a lot of money — but only if the phone is sound. A phone that looks great in photos can hide a failing battery, a locked screen, or worse, a stolen-phone block that makes it a brick the day after you buy it.

The good news: with a ten-minute checklist, you can check almost everything that matters before you pay. Here's the full walkthrough, in the order you should do it.

## What you'll need

- The phone itself, fully charged if possible
- Your own SIM card (to test calls and data), or at least a Wi-Fi connection
- The seller's original purchase info, if they have it
- A quiet spot where you can actually test it

## The physical check

### Screen

Look at the screen from several angles on a bright, white background. What you're hunting for:

- **Cracks, scratches, and dead pixels** — dots that stay black or a fixed color.
- **Burn-in** — faint ghost images left by a screen left on the same app for months.
- **Touch issues** — drag your finger across the whole screen and check for dead zones, and make sure the edges register taps.

### Buttons, ports, and speakers

- Test every button: power, volume, home (if any), and any side buttons.
- Check the charging port with a cable — the port is a common failure point, so make sure the cable clicks in firmly and charges.
- Play audio: loud enough and clear from the bottom speaker, and if there's a headphone jack, test it too.

### Cameras

- Open the camera app and check both lenses. Take a photo against a bright background and one against a dark one — look for blur, spots, and focus issues.
- Switch to the front camera and check the selfie lens, plus the microphone during a voice or video recording.

### Charging

Plug it in and confirm it actually charges. This catches both a faulty port and a battery that won't hold a charge.

## Check the battery health

This is the check that matters most, because a worn battery means a phone that dies by afternoon and costs money to fix.

### On iPhone

Open **Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging**, and look at **Maximum Capacity**. A high figure means a healthy battery; a noticeably lower one means the battery has wear, so factor a repair or replacement into the price. If the screen says the battery is "Unknown" or "Service," treat it as a worn battery.

### On Android

Android doesn't have one universal battery-health screen, but most brands hide it somewhere in settings — look under **Settings → Battery** and tap any "Battery health," "Battery information," or similar option (the location varies by brand). If the phone doesn't show it, an app can give an estimate, but take app estimates as a rough guide rather than gospel. Alternatively, just watch the phone: does it lose a lot of charge quickly from a full battery?

## Software and status checks

### Activation lock / Find My

A phone still linked to someone else's Apple or Google account is the fastest way to get locked out after purchase. On an iPhone, check **Settings → your name** and make sure the seller can sign out of the account in front of you. On Android, check the security and accounts settings for any active account, and confirm "Find My Device" is off after they sign out. Never buy a phone the seller can't sign out of on the spot.

### Carrier lock

If the phone was sold locked to one carrier, it may not work with yours. Ask, then check in **Settings → General → About** (iPhone) or the network settings (Android) for any carrier or SIM lock message. If you can, put your own SIM in and make a call to be sure.

### The IMEI number

The IMEI is the phone's unique serial number — you'll find it by dialing *#06# or in the About settings. Running it through the manufacturer's or a carrier's IMEI-checker can reveal whether the phone is reported lost or stolen. A phone that comes back "blocked" or "blacklisted" is not worth any price, because it may not work on any network.

## The handover

Once everything checks out:

1. **Watch the seller sign out** of their Apple or Google account and disable any anti-theft features while you're present.
2. **Reset the phone to factory settings** (or confirm the seller does) so you start clean.
3. **Ask for the original box and receipt** if available — they help with warranty and resale later.
4. **Pay in a way that leaves a trail** — card or an app payment rather than cash, especially for pricier phones.

## Meeting safely

Meet in a public place — a carrier store is ideal, because staff can often help verify the IMEI. Bring your own SIM to test the network, and don't be rushed: a seller who pressures you to skip checks is a reason to walk away.

## Wrapping up

A used phone is a great deal when it passes the checklist: sound screen, working buttons and cameras, a battery you can live with, no account locks, and a clean IMEI. Run the whole thing before you pay, and you've turned the riskiest purchase in tech into a safe one.
