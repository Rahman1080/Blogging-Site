The good news first: deleting a photo almost never erases it instantly. On both Android and iPhone, deleted photos sit in a holding area for around 30 days, so recovery is usually a few taps. The honest catch: after that window, or if the photo never made it into a backup, it's very likely gone for good.

Here's the real recovery playbook, platform by platform, plus why you should ignore the "recovery app" ads.

## First, the honest truth about deleted photos

When you delete a photo, your phone doesn't wipe the data on the spot. It moves the photo to a trash-like folder — **Recently Deleted** on iPhone, **Trash** in Google Photos and on most Android phones — where it stays for roughly 30 days. During that time, restoring is trivial.

Two things end that easy window:

- **The 30 days expire.** After that, the holding area empties itself automatically.
- **You empty it manually.** Many people delete photos to [free up storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone), not realizing they have to empty the holding area to actually reclaim the space — which also ends any chance of undoing a mistake.

So the question is always the same: *was the photo in the holding area, and is that window still open?*

## Recover deleted photos on an iPhone

### Restore from Recently Deleted

This is your first and best option, and it works for up to 30 days after deletion:

1. Open the **Photos** app.
2. Tap **Albums**, then scroll to **Recently Deleted** (under Utilities).
3. Tap **Select** in the top-right corner.
4. Choose the photos you want back, then tap **Recover** and confirm.

The photos return to your main library exactly where they were. If you deleted a *lot* and your storage is tight, check the Related questions — emptying Recently Deleted is the fastest way to reclaim space, but only do it once you're sure.

### Recover from an iCloud backup

If a photo isn't in Recently Deleted, your next hope is a backup that captured it before deletion:

- If **iCloud Photos** is on, photos are stored in iCloud and sync across your devices. Log into `iCloud.com` and check the Photos app there — deleted-and-expired photos from *this* device may still exist on another synced device or in iCloud's copy.
- If you have a full **iCloud backup** of the phone from before the deletion, you can restore that backup to the phone. This rolls the whole device back, though, so it's only worth it if the photos matter more than the other data you'd lose.

Our [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) walks through both setups and how to keep them healthy.

## Recover deleted photos on Android

### Restore from Google Photos Trash

If your Android phone uses **Google Photos** for gallery backup:

1. Open **Google Photos**.
2. Tap the **menu** (three lines) and choose **Trash**.
3. Select the photos you want and tap **Restore**.

Deleted photos stay in Trash for **60 days** in Google Photos — a little more generous than the phone's own folder. Restored photos go back into your library with their original date.

### Check the phone's own trash or gallery folder

Some phones keep a second, local holding folder in the stock gallery app:

1. Open your phone's **Gallery** or **Photos** app (the one that came with the phone, if you don't use Google Photos).
2. Look for a folder called **Trash**, **Recently Deleted**, or **Bin**.
3. Select and **Restore** the photos you need.

Phone makers (Samsung, Xiaomi, and others) each name this folder slightly differently, but the idea is the same — and the time limit is usually around 30 days.

### Look for a backup from the phone's maker

Some Android makers include their own cloud backup with your account (Samsung Cloud is the common example). If the photos were backed up there before deletion:

1. Sign in to the maker's cloud service on the web or on the phone.
2. Browse the photo backup and look for the missing images.
3. Restore or download the ones you need.

It's not universal, but if it exists on your phone it's worth a look before you give up.

## Why recovery apps are usually a scam

You've seen the ads: "Recover deleted photos instantly!" Here's why they don't work.

- **Modern phones encrypt stored data and reuse freed space quickly.** By the time you install the app, the deleted photo's data has usually been overwritten. There's nothing left to scan for.
- **The legitimate recovery window is the holding area** — which you can access for free, without an app, in the steps above.
- **Many of these apps are actively harmful.** They demand payment, or permissions like full photo access and contact lists, which they then use for their own purposes.

The only "recovery" tools that genuinely work are forensic ones used by professionals on untouched storage — not something a phone app can do. Save your money.

## How to keep this from happening again

The permanent fix is boring but effective: **turn on automatic photo backup.**

- **Android:** Google Photos → your account → **Back up & sync** (or **Backup**), on.
- **iPhone:** Settings → your name → **iCloud → Photos**, toggle **Sync this iPhone** on.

Once photos are in the cloud before you delete them, a mistaken deletion is an inconvenience, not a disaster — the photo is still in your backup even after the phone's 30-day window. It also means deleting photos to free space actually works, because you're not losing the only copy. Both platforms even let you free up on-device space while keeping the full-resolution version in the cloud.

## Wrapping up

Your deleted photos live in a 30-day holding area — check Recently Deleted on iPhone, Trash in Google Photos, and any phone-maker trash folder on Android, and restore before the clock runs out. Past that window, only a backup can save you, and no paid app can change that. Turn on automatic backup now and the next "did I just delete that?" moment turns into a two-second fix.
