Face ID is one of those features you don't appreciate until it stops working. The good news: most Face ID problems are fixable in minutes — a dirty lens, a blocking case, or a face that needs re-enrolling. The bad news: sometimes it's genuinely broken.

Here's the troubleshooting ladder, from "did you clean it" to "call Apple."

## Start here: the quick checks

- **Is the top of your screen clean?** Face ID uses the TrueDepth camera cluster at the top of the phone — a line of lenses and sensors. Fingerprints and smudges there break it instantly. Wipe the top area with a soft, dry cloth.
- **Is there a case or screen protector over it?** Some cases cover the sensor notch, and certain screen protectors (especially privacy or thicker glass) block the infrared scan. Remove the case and see if Face ID starts working.
- **Is your face fully in view?** Face ID needs to see your eyes, nose, and mouth. Hold the phone at arm's length, about a face-width from yours, and look directly at it. Sunglasses can also interfere — take them off for a test.
- **Are you accidentally blocking it?** Your hand or the phone's own orientation can cover the top sensor. Make sure nothing is over the notch.

Half of all Face ID complaints end here. If it's still failing, go deeper.

## Check the Face ID settings

1. Open **Settings → Face ID & Passcode** and enter your passcode.
2. Confirm **iPhone Unlock**, **iTunes & App Store**, and **Password AutoFill** are toggled on. If one is off, Face ID works for the rest but seems "not working" for that specific thing.
3. Check **Require Attention for Face ID**. If it's on, Face ID won't work unless you're actually looking at the screen — fine for most people, but if you wear heavy glasses or hold the phone oddly, it can cause constant failures. Turn it off to relax the requirement.
4. If you wear a mask often, enable **Face ID with Mask** (on the iPhone models that support it) so the phone scans around your eyes instead of demanding your full face.

## Re-enroll your face

Sometimes the phone's stored scan has gone stale — your appearance changed, lighting conditions changed, or the enrollment got corrupted. Re-enrolling fixes most lingering problems.

1. Go to **Settings → Face ID & Passcode** and enter your passcode.
2. Tap **Reset Face ID**.
3. Tap **Set Up Face ID** and scan your face again. Hold the phone at eye level and slowly move your head in a circle when prompted, a second time if asked.
4. Optionally, tap **Set Up an Alternate Appearance** to add a second look — useful if you wear glasses or have grown a beard since.

Re-enrolling is safe and fast, and it genuinely fixes the "Face ID stopped working out of nowhere" case more often than people expect.

## Restart and update

- **Restart the iPhone.** Hold the side button and a volume button, slide to power off, and turn it back on. This clears whatever temporary state was blocking the camera system.
- **Update iOS.** **Settings → General → Software Update.** Face ID uses a secure component called the Secure Enclave, and Apple's updates include compatibility fixes. If Face ID stopped working right after an update, a newer update often repairs it.

If Face ID says **"Face ID is not available"** specifically — rather than failing to match your face — the camera system isn't responding at all, which brings us to the honest part.

## The honest part: when it's hardware

Some Face ID failures have no software fix. The TrueDepth system is precision hardware, and these are the real-world causes:

- **A drop.** Even a modest drop can knock the camera array out of alignment while the screen looks perfectly fine. This is the most common hardware cause.
- **Water or liquid damage.**
- **A screen repair** done with a non-genuine part or done incorrectly can disable Face ID entirely — it's notorious for this.

The test: if Face ID fails after you've cleaned everything, removed accessories, re-enrolled, restarted, and updated — and especially if it says "not available" — it's almost certainly hardware. **No setting, restart, or reset will fix a hardware fault.** Book an Apple appointment and let them diagnose it. If your phone is covered by AppleCare, a repair may be cheap or free; if not, the service cost is what it is.

## Keep your passcode strong anyway

Here's the part people forget: Face ID is a convenience, and your **passcode is the real security**. Every Face ID failure falls back to the passcode — swipe up and enter it. Apple also designed it so that entering your passcode causes Face ID to re-learn your appearance and often start working again.

That's exactly why your passcode should be strong and private: it's the one thing that always works, even if Face ID never does again. ## Wrapping up

Clean the lens, remove the case, check the settings, re-enroll your face, restart, update — in that order. That fixes the overwhelming majority of Face ID problems. If it still says "not available" after all that, it's hardware, and the honest answer is an Apple appointment, not more restarting.
