A slow Android phone is frustrating, but in most cases it's fixable without buying a new device. The causes are usually the same handful of things: not enough free storage, too many background apps, outdated software, or a battery that's struggling.

Below are ten fixes, ordered from quickest and safest to more involved. Work through them in order — most people solve the problem within the first few steps.

## Before you start

- Back up important photos and files before clearing anything.
- Make sure your phone is charged above 50% (plug it in if it isn't).
- Note your phone's brand and Android version — steps below are general, but settings names vary slightly between Samsung, Pixel, Xiaomi, and others.

## 1. Restart your phone

The simplest fix is also the most effective. A restart clears temporary memory, ends stuck background processes, and stops apps that are misbehaving.

1. Press and hold the power button.
2. Select **Restart** (or turn it off and on again).
3. Wait a minute and use the phone normally.

If your phone was fine for months and suddenly got slow, restart it before doing anything else.

## 2. Free up storage space

Android phones slow down noticeably when storage is nearly full — the system needs free space to write temporary files and run smoothly.

1. Open **Settings → Storage**.
2. Check how much free space you have. Aim for at least 10–15% free.
3. Clear out the biggest offenders: old photos and videos (back them up to Google Photos first), downloaded files, and apps you no longer use.
4. Use **Settings → Storage → Smart Storage / Free up space** if your phone offers it.

> Tip: If you store hundreds of videos, moving them to Google Photos or a cloud drive is the fastest way to reclaim space.

## 3. Clear app caches

Cached data helps apps open faster, but an overgrown cache can cause lag.

1. Open **Settings → Apps** (called "Apps & notifications" on some phones).
2. Tap the largest apps one by one.
3. Tap **Storage & cache → Clear cache**. Don't tap "Clear storage" — that deletes the app's data and logs you out.

Doing this for your browser, social media, and photo apps often gives an instant speed boost.

## 4. Update your apps and Android version

Updates fix bugs, patch security holes, and often include performance improvements.

- Open the **Google Play Store → Manage apps** and tap **Update all**.
- Check for system updates: **Settings → System → Software update / System update**.
- Restart your phone after a big update.

An old Android version running newer apps is a common recipe for slowness.

## 5. Limit background apps

Too many apps running in the background drains memory and battery.

- Open **Settings → Battery** and review which apps use the most.
- Use **Battery → Background usage limits / Restrict background activity** for apps you don't need running constantly (games, video apps).
- On Samsung phones, use **Settings → Battery and device care → Memory** to see memory pressure.

Don't obsess over this step — modern Android manages background apps well. Only restrict the obvious offenders.

## 6. Remove bloatware and unused apps

Pre-installed "bloatware" and abandoned apps you never open still consume updates and memory.

1. Open **Settings → Apps → All apps**.
2. Uninstall apps you haven't used in months.
3. For apps you can't uninstall, tap **Disable** instead — this stops them running without removing them.

## 7. Check for overheating

Phones deliberately slow down (throttle) when they get hot to protect the hardware. If your phone is warm to the touch, that's likely why it's slow.

- Remove thick cases while charging or gaming.
- Avoid charging while using demanding apps.
- Keep the phone out of direct sunlight.
- If it's hot even when idle, the battery may be failing — see step 9.

## 8. Run a quick security check

Malware and ad-heavy apps can secretly consume resources and slow a phone down.

- Stick to the Google Play Store for downloads.
- Check installed apps for anything unfamiliar under **Settings → Apps**.
- Use Play Protect: open **Play Store → Profile → Play Protect** and run a scan.
- Remove any app you don't remember installing.

## 9. Consider the battery

An aging or swollen battery can cause slowdowns, random shutdowns, and overheating.

- If your battery drains unusually fast or the phone shuts off above 1%, it may be worn.
- A genuine replacement battery is far cheaper than a new phone.
- See our [guide to making your Android battery last longer](/articles/extend-android-battery-life) for day-to-day tips.

## 10. Factory reset — the last resort

If nothing above helps, a factory reset restores the phone to a clean state. This wipes everything, so back up first.

1. Back up to Google (Settings → Accounts → Google) and Google Photos.
2. Open **Settings → System → Reset options → Erase all data (factory reset)**.
3. Set the phone up fresh and install only the apps you actually use.

A reset almost always resolves slowdowns caused by accumulated junk or corrupted settings.

## When it's time to upgrade

If your phone is 4–5+ years old, no longer receives updates, or has hardware problems, no amount of cleaning will fix it. Before spending money, read our [laptop and computer buying guide](/articles/how-to-choose-a-laptop-buying-guide) — the same principles of "buy what fits your needs" apply to phones. If you're buying, focus on one with at least 128 GB of storage and a recent mid-range chip; that combination stays fast for years.

## Wrapping up

Start with the easy wins — restart, free up storage, clear caches — and you'll fix most slow phones in under ten minutes. Save the factory reset for when you really need it, and know when it's genuinely time to upgrade.
