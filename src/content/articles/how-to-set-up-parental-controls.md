Parental controls are easier to set up than most people expect. Both Android and iPhone have free, built-in systems — **Family Link** on Android, **Screen Time** on iPhone — that let you limit screen time, approve apps, filter content, and lock down purchases without buying anything extra.

Here's how to set both up, and what each system can actually do.

## What parental controls can (and can't) do

Worth being honest about before you start: parental controls are a tool, not a babysitter. They manage *where* the phone lets a child go — which apps, which sites, how long — but they can't teach a child to spot a scam. That part is conversation, and our guides on [AI scam calls and deepfakes](/articles/ai-scams-deepfake-calls) and [identifying AI scam messages](/articles/how-to-identify-ai-scam-message) are good starting points to share with them.

What the controls *can* do:

- Limit how long apps and the whole device are used.
- Schedule downtime (bedtime hours when only calls work, for example).
- Approve or block app downloads.
- Set content and age ratings for the app store.
- Require your approval for purchases.

## On Android: Google Family Link

Family Link is Google's free parental control app. You manage everything from your own phone, and the child gets their own Google account if they don't have one.

### 1. Download and set up Family Link

1. Install **Family Link** on your phone from the Google Play Store.
2. Open it and tap **Get started**.
3. Choose **Parent** and sign in with your Google account.
4. Follow the prompts to create your child's Google account, or add an existing one.

There's a verification step where you confirm you're the parent — you may need to enter your account password or a card detail as proof of age. This is Google's way of making sure kids don't set up a "parent" account themselves.

### 2. Set the basics

From the Family Link app on your phone you can now control:

- **Screen time.** Set a daily limit, or a schedule for school days versus weekends.
- **Bedtime.** Choose hours when the phone goes quiet overnight.
- **App limits.** Restrict specific apps, or give extra time on homework apps.

### 3. Approve apps and purchases

In the Family Link app:

1. Tap your child's name.
2. Choose **Controls → Apps** (or **Google Play** settings).
3. Turn on **App approvals** so every new app download sends a request to your phone.
4. Set the **Content restrictions** age rating that filters the Play Store.
5. Turn on purchase approval for the Play Store and Google Play, so nothing is bought without you.

If a child asks for an app you don't love, you can block it outright or approve it with a conversation attached. You can also lock the child's device remotely from the app if it's lost or it's bedtime and they're not complying.

## On iPhone: Screen Time

Screen Time is built into iOS, and it gets more powerful when you pair it with **Family Sharing**. The simplest way to manage a child's iPhone: set it up with their Apple ID as a child in your family group.

### 1. Turn on Screen Time

- **On your own iPhone:** Go to **Settings → Screen Time → Family Sharing** and choose the child. From there you can manage everything remotely.
- **On the child's iPhone:** Go to **Settings → Screen Time**, turn it on, then follow the prompt to set it up *for a child* and enter your own device's Screen Time passcode.

The passcode is the key: it stops the child from changing the controls. Don't use their birthday.

### 2. Set limits and downtime

In Screen Time on your phone (or the child's):

- **Downtime** — pick hours when only approved apps and calls work (bedtime, homework time, whatever suits your family).
- **App Limits** — set daily caps for whole categories (Social, Games) or individual apps.

### 3. Approve apps and purchases

1. In **Screen Time**, go to **Content & Privacy Restrictions** and turn it on.
2. Set **Content Restrictions** — age ratings for apps, movies, music, and websites.
3. Under **iTunes & App Store Purchases**, set **Installing Apps**, **Deleting Apps**, and **In-app Purchases** to **Ask to Buy** or block them.

With Family Sharing and Ask to Buy, the child's app and in-app purchase requests land on your device for approval — the same system that stops surprise credit-card charges.

## What both systems share

No matter which platform you picked:

- **Both are free and built-in.** You don't need a third-party parental control subscription for the basics.
- **Both let you manage remotely** — your phone is the control panel for your child's device.
- **Both have a PIN/passcode wall** so the child can't undo the settings.
- **Both cover the phone, not the whole house.** A kid with a tablet, a school laptop, or a friend's phone is outside the controls. Set expectations about that rather than pretending the limits are universal.

## The honest limits

- **In-app purchases and game stores** can have their own logins and payment methods. Set purchase approval where you can, but also agree on rules about buying in games.
- **Websites change fast.** Content filters catch a lot but not everything, and kids who really want around a filter will find a way. Controls reduce exposure; they don't replace a conversation about what to do when something weird or scary shows up.
- **Every child is different.** The settings above are a starting point, not a one-size-fits-all verdict. Most families adjust downtime and app limits as kids grow, and that's the system working as intended.

## Wrapping up

Family Link and Screen Time give you real, free control over what a child's phone can do — limits, approvals, content filters, and purchase locks, all managed from your own device.
