The honest answer: a tablet can replace a laptop for many people, but only if your laptop job is light enough. For students, travelers, and people whose work is browsing, email, documents, and video calls, a tablet with a keyboard case genuinely gets the job done. For serious multitasking, desktop software, or heavy typing, a laptop still wins.

Here's the real comparison — what tablets do brilliantly, where they still fall short, and a decision rule that works for almost anyone.

## What tablets do really well

### Portability and battery

A tablet is lighter, thinner, and starts instantly. It's the thing you carry to the couch, the classroom, the coffee shop, or the plane without thinking about it. Battery life is typically excellent, and because there's no fan and no heavy hardware, it sips power.

### Media, reading, and casual browsing

For streaming, reading, browsing, and games, a tablet is the comfortable, immediate choice — arguably better than a laptop, because you can hold it, prop it up, or hand it over.

### Note-taking and drawing

This is the tablet's standout job. With a stylus, a tablet becomes a genuinely good digital notebook and sketchpad — something a laptop simply can't do naturally. If your day involves handwritten notes, diagrams, or drawing, a tablet has a real edge.

## Where tablets still fall short

### Desktop apps and software

This is the big one. The apps a tablet runs are the touch-optimized versions — often with fewer features than the desktop equivalents. If your work or school relies on software that's only available (or only fully featured) on a computer, a tablet can't replace your laptop. Check the exact apps you use before you decide.

### Multitasking

Laptops let you put windows side by side and switch freely. Tablets can do split-screen these days, but it's more cramped and less fluid, especially on a smaller screen. If you live in five tabs and three apps at once, you'll feel the squeeze.

### Keyboards and typing

A keyboard case is better than on-screen typing but still not a laptop keyboard. It's usually smaller, with less travel, and the setup — a tablet balanced on a thin case — is less stable on your lap. If you type a lot, a laptop is still the more comfortable tool. Our [keyboard comparison](/articles/mechanical-vs-membrane-keyboard) explains why the feel of typing matters for long sessions.

### Ports, repairs, and longevity

Tablets are harder to repair, have fewer ports (often just one USB-C), and you can't upgrade the storage or RAM later. Laptops — especially ones chosen with an eye to [the specs that matter](/articles/how-to-choose-a-laptop-buying-guide) — tend to age better as a work machine.

## The keyboard case question

The phrase "tablet with a keyboard" describes two very different things:

- **A simple keyboard case** — thin, light, fine for short emails and notes. Think of it as typing with training wheels.
- **A detachable keyboard tablet** — the tablet bolts onto a real keyboard deck, making a device that's closer to a small laptop while still popping free as a tablet.

Detachable keyboard models genuinely close most of the gap. What they still don't give you is the stable lap typing, the full desktop software, and the comfortable multitasking of a laptop.

## Match it to your main task

| Your main task | Pick |
| --- | --- |
| Streaming, reading, browsing, note-taking | Tablet |
| Handwritten notes, diagrams, drawing | Tablet with stylus |
| Email, documents, spreadsheets, video calls | Either — keyboard case or budget laptop |
| Desktop software, coding, heavy multitasking | Laptop |
| Serious gaming | Laptop |
| School with mixed classes | Either — see below |

### For students

If a class requires desktop-only software or lots of typing, a laptop is the safer, more capable bet. If your work is reading, note-taking, and light documents — and you already have a computer at home — a tablet with a stylus is a fantastic companion. Many students end up with both, using each for what it does best. If you're choosing just one for study, our [guide to choosing a computer for school](/articles/how-to-choose-computer-college) walks through the factors.

### For work

If your job is defined by desktop apps, meetings, and typing — laptop. If your work is mostly communication, browsing, and light documents — a tablet with a keyboard case can genuinely replace it, especially when paired with the cloud.

## The decision rule

Answer one question: **what will you do with it most, and will a tablet do that job fully?**

- If your heaviest tasks are media, notes, and light work — and the tablet runs the apps you need — a tablet saves money, weight, and space.
- If your heaviest task is anything that needs desktop software, comfortable typing, or serious multitasking — get a laptop.

And if you're still torn, remember the middle path: a mid-range laptop plus a budget tablet often costs less than one premium laptop and covers both worlds.

## Wrapping up

In 2026, tablets are closer to laptops than ever, and for light work they genuinely replace them. The deciding factors are software, typing, and multitasking: if your daily apps run fully on a tablet and you don't type all day, a tablet with a keyboard case is the pick. Otherwise, a laptop — matched to your usage with our [laptop buying guide](/articles/how-to-choose-a-laptop-buying-guide) — is still the more capable machine.
