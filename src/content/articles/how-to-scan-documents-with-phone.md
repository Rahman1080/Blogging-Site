# How to Scan Documents With Your Phone (Use It as a Scanner)

Your phone already has a perfectly good scanner, and you've been carrying it for years. Every modern iPhone and Android phone can turn a sheet of paper into a clean PDF using tools that are already installed — no download, no paid scanner app, no trip to a copier.

Signing a lease, submitting expenses, or mailing a form back is a two-minute job once you know where the scanner lives. Here's how to use the built-in scanners on iPhone and Android, combine pages into one PDF, and send the result.

## Scan documents on iPhone with Notes

Apple builds a scanner into the **Notes** app, and it's the fastest way to scan on an iPhone.

1. Open **Notes** and tap the **new note** button (or open an existing note you want to add the scan to).
2. Tap the **camera icon** above the keyboard, then choose **Scan Documents**.
3. Hold the phone over the document. The camera auto-detects the page edges and captures it — you'll hear a shutter sound. It can capture multiple pages this way; just keep the phone in the scan screen and move to the next page.
4. When you're done, tap **Save** (or **Keep Scan**).
5. Tap the scan in your note to crop, rotate, or apply a color filter before saving.

The scan is saved to the note as a PDF you can share, email, or export. To send it: tap the scan, tap the **share icon**, and pick your email app or **Save to Files**.

## Scan documents on Android with Google Drive

Android's built-in scanner lives in **Google Drive**, which is installed on most Android phones by default.

1. Open **Google Drive**.
2. Tap the **+** button in the bottom right, then tap **Scan**.
3. Point the camera at the document. Drive auto-detects the edges and shows a preview with a blue boundary. Adjust the corners if it got them slightly wrong.
4. Tap the shutter to capture. For multi-page documents, keep scanning — each page is added to the same document.
5. Tap **Save** and choose a name and folder. The result is a PDF saved to Drive.

On some phones, Google's **Files** app has the same capability hidden behind a "Scan a document" shortcut, and Google Lens can also extract text from a document photo. But Drive is the dependable, always-there option.

## Turn multi-page scans into one PDF

Scanners handle this for you — the trick is not to exit the scan screen between pages.

1. Scan the first page normally.
2. Before tapping Save, move the phone over the next page and tap the shutter again.
3. Repeat for every page, then Save.

The pages combine into a single PDF automatically. Most built-in scanners let you tap and hold a page thumbnail to reorder pages or delete a bad one before saving. If you scanned pages separately, both Notes and Drive let you combine them later — in Drive, select the PDFs, tap the three-dot menu, and look for "Merge" (available in the Drive app on phones).

## Scan and email a document

Once you have the PDF, sending it is plain file attachment.

- **On iPhone:** tap the scan, tap the **share icon**, choose **Mail**, and the PDF attaches automatically. Or save it to Files first (share icon → Save to Files) and attach it from there.
- **On Android:** open your email app, start a new message, tap the **paperclip**, and browse to the PDF in Drive, Downloads, or Files.

For a contract or form you need to sign, most scanner tools also let you export a photo of a signature and drop it onto the PDF — but the simplest route is to use your email app's built-in markup or a free PDF editor.

## Get a clean scan every time

Scan quality is mostly about the setup, not the app. Four habits make the difference:

1. **Flat surface, good light.** Put the document on a table with even light. A spot near a window beats a dim room, and it prevents the shadows that make text hard to read.
2. **Fill the frame.** Hold the phone directly above the document, not at an angle. The auto-detection straightens it, but it can't fix a photo taken sideways.
3. **Steady hands.** Lean your elbows on the table or brace the phone against something. A blurry scan is unfixable; a steady one is instantly readable.
4. **Watch your shadow.** Scan from directly above so your hand and phone don't cast a shadow across the page.

The built-in scanners auto-crop, auto-straighten, and apply a color filter by default (usually "color" or "photo"). For text documents, choosing the **Black & White** or **Grayscale** filter shrinks the file dramatically and makes it print better.

## Why you don't need a paid scanner app

Scanner apps on the store promise OCR (making scanned text searchable), cloud sync, and fancy filters. The honest trade-off: the built-in tools cover signing, emailing, storing, and printing — the things people actually do — for free and with no ads. The built-in scanners on both platforms even include basic OCR: scanned documents in Notes and Drive are text-searchable in many cases.

Reach for a dedicated scanner app only if you have a specific need the built-ins don't meet, like batch-scanning hundreds of pages into an organized archive. For everything else, the tools already on your phone are more than enough — and they keep your data on your own accounts instead of a third-party service.

Your phone is a real document scanner. Scan with Notes on iPhone or Google Drive on Android, let the auto-detection square up the page, combine multiple pages into one PDF, and email it straight from the share menu. No new apps, no cost, no learning curve — just a better way to handle paperwork.
