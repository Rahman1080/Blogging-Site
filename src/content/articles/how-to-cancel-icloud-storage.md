iCloud gives you 5 GB free, and for most people that vanishes within weeks. The paid tiers — commonly 50 GB, 200 GB, and 2 TB — are easy to sign up for and easy to forget, right up until the renewal email arrives. The good news: changing or cancelling your plan takes about a minute, and nothing you've stored is deleted the moment you do.

Here's exactly how to do it on your iPhone, and the honest version of what happens to your data when you step down.

## Before you cancel: check what you're using

A downgrade is painless when your data fits the smaller plan, and annoying when it doesn't. So check first.

1. Open **Settings → your name → iCloud**.
2. Tap **Manage Account Storage** (it may appear as "iCloud Storage" on some iOS versions).
3. Look at the bar showing what's using space — **Photos** and **Backups** are almost always the two big ones.
4. Now compare: if your current usage is, say, 90 GB and you're dropping to 50 GB, you have homework to do before you downgrade. Our [phone storage guide](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) shows how to shrink that number.

You don't need everything to fit before you click — but knowing the gap in advance means no surprises later.

## How to change or cancel your plan on iPhone

This is the standard route, and it works on current and recent iOS versions.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **your name** at the top.
3. Tap **iCloud**.
4. Tap **Manage Account Storage**.
5. Tap **Change Storage Plan** (if you're on the free tier, it's worded as "Buy More Storage" or similar).
6. Scroll to the bottom and tap **Downgrade Options**.
7. Choose the smaller plan you want — including the **free 5 GB** option if you're cancelling the subscription entirely.
8. Confirm, sign in with your Apple ID password if asked, and the change is submitted.

If you're paying through a family group, the plan is shared and owned by the family organizer — you'll see a note about it in the same screen, and only the organizer can change it.

## The web route (if you're not near your phone)

You can also manage the plan from any computer:

1. Sign in at **icloud.com** (or **appleid.apple.com**) with your Apple ID.
2. Open **Account Settings**, then find **Manage** under storage or iCloud.
3. Choose **Downgrade** or the smaller plan and follow the prompts.

It's the same account, so whichever route you use, the change shows up on all your devices.

## What actually happens when you downgrade

This is where people get anxious, so here's the honest picture. **Nothing is deleted the moment you downgrade.** Apple doesn't wipe your photos or files. What happens is subtler:

### Backups stop completing

Your iCloud Backup has to fit inside your available storage. Downgrade below your backup's size and **new backups stop** — the phone simply can't write them. Your last successful backup stays on iCloud, but it's frozen in time. If you lose your phone a month later, you restore from that older backup. This is exactly why the [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) suggests a computer backup as a safety net.

### iCloud Photos pauses syncing

If your photo library exceeds the plan, iCloud Photos **stops syncing**: new photos don't upload, and edits don't propagate to your other devices. Your originals already in iCloud stay safe — you're just on pause. Free up space (or upgrade) and syncing resumes.

### iCloud Mail needs room

This is the one that surprises people. iCloud Mail takes storage too, and if there isn't enough room for it after a downgrade, **Apple can disable iCloud Mail** — and the address can be lost if it stays disabled for long. If you rely on an @icloud.com address, check your mail usage in Manage Account Storage before cancelling, and move it if you're close to the edge.

### Your 5 GB free tier is always there

Cancel the paid plan entirely and you drop to the free 5 GB. Everything that fits keeps working; everything that doesn't goes on pause as described above.

## What if you want a bigger plan instead?

The same screen handles upgrades, and they're even simpler — there's nothing to check first because you're buying more room, not less.

1. **Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage**.
2. Tap **Change Storage Plan**.
3. Pick the larger tier and confirm with your Apple ID password (or Face ID / Touch ID).

Upgrades apply immediately and are prorated — you're charged for the difference for the rest of the billing period rather than paying for a whole new month. If your family is bumping into the limit together, it's usually worth buying one bigger shared plan than several small ones.

## Family Sharing and iCloud storage

If you're in a Family Sharing group, the storage plan works a little differently:

- A paid iCloud+ plan is **shared by the whole family**, and it's owned and paid for by the family organizer. Everyone's iCloud data counts against that one plan's total.
- **Only the organizer can change or cancel the plan.** If you're a family member and want a different plan, you'd have to leave the family group or buy your own separate plan.
- If you're the organizer and you downgrade, everyone in the family is affected at once — their backups and photo syncs pause too if the shared total exceeds the new plan.

If you're a member and the family plan feels wrong for you, the cleanest option is your own paid plan rather than fighting over the shared one. It's a separate purchase under your own Apple ID and doesn't touch anyone else's storage.

## When the change takes effect

Upgrades are usually immediate. **Downgrades can show as pending until the end of your current billing cycle** — you've paid for the bigger plan until then, so it stays available. Either way, you'll see the new plan reflected in Settings once it processes, and Apple won't charge you again after the downgrade takes hold.

## Wrapping up

Changing your iCloud plan is a one-minute job: Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Change Storage Plan → Downgrade Options. Check your usage first, be honest about the two big space hogs (photos and backups), and remember that downgrading pauses things rather than deleting them — until it doesn't.
