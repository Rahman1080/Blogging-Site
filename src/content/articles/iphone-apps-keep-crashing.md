Apps crash. It's annoying, but it's almost never the phone's fault — and almost always fixable in a few minutes. Here's the ladder to work through, from the quickest fix to the deepest one.

The first thing to notice: is it **one app** or **many**? One app crashing means that app has a bug — not your phone. Many apps crashing at once points to something on the phone itself, like low storage or a pending iOS issue. That distinction tells you how far down this list you need to go.

## 1. Restart the iPhone

The classic first step, and it genuinely fixes a surprising number of crashes.

1. Hold the **side button** and either volume button.
2. Slide to **power off**.
3. Wait a few seconds, then hold the side button to turn it back on.

A restart clears corrupted temporary state and gives every app a clean slate. If crashes persist, move on.

## 2. Update the crashing app

A crashing app is often running an outdated or freshly broken version. Developers push fixes for crash bugs all the time.

1. Open the **App Store**.
2. Tap your **profile icon** in the top-right.
3. Scroll to see pending updates, or tap **Update All**. Find the crashing app and update it if there's an update.

Check whether the crash started right after a recent app update — if so, the update introduced the bug, and the fix may be in the next update, or the app's developer may have posted about it.

## 3. Update iOS

Sometimes the app is fine and iOS is the problem. Apple's updates regularly include fixes for crashes and compatibility issues.

1. **Settings → General → Software Update**.
2. Install anything available. Your phone will restart as part of the update.

An iOS update is also a good fix for crashes that started after a previous update went wrong.

## 4. Offload or reinstall the app

If a single app still crashes after updates, give it a clean reinstall.

**The gentle option — Offload App:** in **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**, tap the crashing app and choose **Offload App**. This deletes the app but keeps its documents and data, so you can reinstall from the App Store without losing anything. Try this first.

**The thorough option — delete and reinstall:** tap and hold the app icon on the Home Screen, tap **Remove App → Delete App**, then reinstall it from the App Store. This wipes the app's local data, so make sure anything important in the app is backed up to iCloud or the app's own account first. The [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) explains what iCloud protects.

## 5. Check storage

Low storage causes crashes across the board — apps can't write temporary files, and the system starts killing things to survive.

1. Go to **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**.
2. If the bar is nearly full, free space. Empty **Recently Deleted** photos, offload unused apps, and clear old downloads.

Our [phone storage guide](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) has the full walkthrough. If storage was the problem, crashes often stop immediately once there's room to breathe.

## 6. Reset All Settings

If multiple apps keep crashing and everything else has failed, reset the system settings. This is deeper than a restart but still **doesn't erase your data** — it resets preferences like Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth devices, wallpaper, and notification settings.

1. **Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Reset → Reset All Settings**.
2. Confirm, and enter your passcode if asked.
3. The phone restarts. You'll need to rejoin your Wi-Fi networks afterward — a small price for a clean slate.

If you get to this step, it's also worth noting this is a good moment to make sure your iPhone is backed up, in case you want to go further later.

## If one specific action always crashes the app

Sometimes the app works fine until you do one thing — open a particular chat, export a long video, scan a document — and then it dies. That's usually not random flakiness; it's the app hitting its own limit, and it tells you where the problem is.

- **It crashes on a huge file or long chat:** the app is running out of memory for that one piece of content. Freeing phone storage and closing other heavy apps can help, but the realistic fix is to break the task up (open the chat differently, export in shorter clips) or wait for the developer to optimize.
- **It crashes only right after launch:** that's a corrupted install or a version bug, and the reinstall step above is the reliable fix.
- **It crashes with the camera or microphone:** check **Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera (or Microphone)** and make sure the app still has permission. An app whose permission was revoked can misbehave instead of asking politely.

None of these mean the phone is failing. They mean the app is doing something the phone can't keep up with, and the fix is usually on the app's side.

## What about apps crashing in a loop?

A crash loop — the app opens, flashes, and closes instantly, over and over — usually responds to the same ladder: update, then reinstall. If reinstalling doesn't break the loop, the app's latest version has a genuine bug that only the developer can fix. Check the App Store page for the app; developers post notes when there's a known issue and a fix incoming. In the meantime, other apps will keep working fine.

## When it's not the apps

Worry about hardware only when the pattern is extreme: every app crashes constantly, the phone restarts on its own, or crashes continue even after you've reset settings and reinstalled the problematic apps. In that rare case, an Apple Store appointment is the honest next step. But for the overwhelmingly common single-app crash? That's just software being software.

## Wrapping up

Restart, update the app, update iOS, reinstall, check storage, reset settings — in that order. Almost every crashing-app problem in the real world is fixed by step 2 or step 4, and none of the early steps put your data at risk.
