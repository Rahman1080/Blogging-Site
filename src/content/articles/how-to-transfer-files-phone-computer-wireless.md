You don't need a cable to move files between your phone and computer anymore. Whatever you're shifting — a handful of photos, a video, a document — there's a wireless route that takes seconds. The trick is picking the right one for your situation.

Here are the practical options, from the set-it-and-forget-it cloud method to the near-instant built-in sharing tools, with an honest note on what's fastest when you're moving a lot.

## What you'll need

For all of these, just two things:

- Your phone and your computer on **the same Wi-Fi network** (for direct transfer methods).
- Both devices signed into the accounts you'll use (your Google or Apple account, or the same cloud storage).

No extra apps required for the built-in methods below.

## Method 1: Cloud storage (easiest, works everywhere)

Cloud storage is the method that never cares what devices you own. The flow is the same on Android, iPhone, Windows, and Mac:

1. Open the cloud app on your phone — **Google Drive**, **iCloud Drive**, **OneDrive**, or **Dropbox**, whichever you use.
2. Tap **Add** or **Upload** and pick the files you want to move.
3. Let them upload. For photos, apps like Google Photos can even upload automatically in the background.
4. On your computer, sign into the same account in the browser or desktop app and download the files — or just access them from the cloud folder that syncs.

Cloud storage is best for: regular transfers, moving files between different brands of devices, and anything you want to reach from multiple places later. It's also a genuine [backup](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) for your important photos, not just a delivery truck.

The honest catch: you're limited by your free storage space, and the upload can take a while for a big batch of videos. For large one-off moves, one of the direct methods below is better.

## Method 2: AirDrop (iPhone to Mac)

If you're moving files between an iPhone and a Mac, AirDrop is the fastest option that exists, and it works without an internet connection.

1. Make sure Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are on for both devices, and they're near each other.
2. On your iPhone, find the file and tap **Share**.
3. Tap **AirDrop** and choose your Mac from the list.
4. On the Mac, click **Accept** when the incoming file appears.

AirDrop doesn't need an account, doesn't compress anything, and moves hundreds of photos in a couple of minutes. The one catch: it's Apple-to-Apple. An iPhone and a Windows PC won't AirDrop to each other. If that's your setup, use cloud storage, or see our guide on [moving photos from iPhone to a computer](/articles/how-to-transfer-photos-iphone-computer) for the cable route.

## Method 3: Nearby Share (Android to Windows or Chromebook)

Android's version of AirDrop has gone by a couple of names — **Nearby Share** and, more recently, **Quick Share** on some devices. Same idea, and it works with Windows PCs and Chromebooks.

1. On your Android phone, find the file and tap **Share**.
2. Choose **Nearby Share** or **Quick Share** from the share sheet.
3. Pick your computer from the list of nearby devices.
4. On the PC or Chromebook, accept the incoming file when prompted.

Like AirDrop, it's direct, fast, and works without the internet. Your phone may need to have location or Bluetooth discovery turned on the first time. On a Windows PC you may need the free Quick Share app installed, which Microsoft and Google both offer for download.

## Method 4: Your phone's own file-sharing tools

Beyond the headline methods, most phones have a general-purpose wireless transfer option built into their files app:

- **iPhone:** In the **Files** app, long-press a file and tap **Share** → **AirDrop** (to a Mac), or use **Save to Files** to push it into iCloud Drive.
- **Android:** In **Files by Google** or your phone's files app, tap **Share** and pick **Nearby Share**, or use a network share option to browse the PC from your phone over the local network.

These are the same tools as above, just reached from a different starting point. If you're organizing files on the computer afterward, our [guide to organizing digital files](/articles/how-to-organize-digital-files) helps turn the pile into something you can actually find later.

## What's fastest for big batches?

Be honest about the trade-off:

- **A few files, any mix** → cloud storage. Fast enough, no setup, works everywhere.
- **A huge batch of photos or videos, Apple to Apple** → AirDrop.
- **A huge batch, Android to Windows/Chromebook** → Nearby Share / Quick Share.
- **The absolute fastest, full resolution, hundreds of gigabytes** → a USB cable still wins. There's no wireless method that beats a cable for sheer speed, and no wireless method that's immune to a slow network.

If you're moving a lot of photos you also want to keep safe, wireless transfer and a proper backup go hand in hand — our [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) explains how to keep that set of photos in two places instead of one.

## The honest limits

Two things people run into, worth knowing up front:

- **Platform walls.** Apple's best tools only talk to Apple. Google's tools are friendly with Windows and Chromebooks but not with iPhones the same way. Cloud storage is the only method that crosses every line.
- **Speed depends on your network.** Direct transfer tools move at Wi-Fi speed, and cloud uploads are capped by your internet upload speed. On a slow connection, even a dozen videos can feel like a wait.

## Wrapping up

Wireless file transfer comes down to one choice: cloud storage for everyday moves across any devices, or a direct tool like AirDrop and Nearby Share when you want speed and you're moving a lot at once.
