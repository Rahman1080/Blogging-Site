Few things are more annoying than a phone that needs charging by early afternoon. Most of the drain comes from a handful of settings and habits you can change in a few minutes.

This guide covers the fixes that genuinely work, in order of impact. Let's start with the biggest wins.

## 1. Check which apps are eating your battery

Before changing anything, find the real culprit.

1. Open **Settings → Battery → Battery usage** (or "Battery and device care" on Samsung).
2. Look at the list of apps and the percentage each uses.
3. Note any app you rarely open but that's using a lot — that's your main target.

The system's own battery screen is far more reliable than any third-party battery app.

## 2. Turn on Battery Saver at the right time

Android's built-in Battery Saver limits background activity, reduces performance slightly, and extends life dramatically.

- Enable it when your battery drops below a level you choose: **Settings → Battery → Battery Saver → Set a schedule** (e.g. turn on at 30%).
- Use the quick toggle in your notification shade when you need it immediately.
- Turn off animations for extra savings: **Settings → Accessibility → Remove animations**.

## 3. Reduce screen brightness and timeout

The screen is usually the biggest battery consumer.

- Lower brightness manually, or enable **Adaptive brightness** (Settings → Display) which learns your preferences.
- Cut the screen timeout to 30 seconds or 1 minute: **Settings → Display → Screen timeout**.
- Use dark mode on OLED screens — **Settings → Display → Dark theme** — where dark pixels use almost no power.

## 4. Stop apps running in the background

Apps that keep checking for updates in the background are silent battery drains.

1. Open **Settings → Battery → Background usage limits** (names vary).
2. For apps you don't need live notifications from (games, shopping apps, video apps), choose **Restrict / Deep sleep**.
3. Leave messaging apps unrestricted so notifications still arrive.

## 5. Manage location services

Location polling is another quiet battery killer.

- Open **Settings → Location → App location permission**.
- Change apps that don't need constant location to **While in use** instead of **Allow all the time**.
- Turn off **Google Location History** if you don't use it: **Settings → Location → Location Services**.

## 6. Fix weak signal issues

If you're somewhere with poor reception, your phone pushes extra power to keep the signal — a huge, invisible drain.

- Use **Wi-Fi calling** where your mobile signal is weak: **Settings → Connections → Wi-Fi calling**.
- In airplane-mode-appropriate situations, connect to Wi-Fi and switch off mobile data.

## 7. Watch out for overheating

Heat is the enemy of battery health and efficiency. A hot phone drains faster and degrades over time.

- Remove thick cases while charging.
- Don't use demanding apps while plugged in.
- Keep the phone out of direct sun in summer.

## 8. Update apps and Android

Updates often include battery-efficiency fixes.

- **Google Play Store → Manage apps → Update all**.
- **Settings → System → System update** for the latest Android version.

## 9. Use adaptive features

Modern Android has several features that learn and optimize:

- **Adaptive Battery** (Settings → Battery) — lets Android prioritize power for apps you actually use.
- **Adaptive Brightness** — keeps screen brightness appropriate to your environment.
- **Doze mode** — works automatically when the phone is still; just don't block it by constantly waking the screen.

## 10. Stop charging from 0% and to 100% (mostly)

Lithium-ion batteries last longest between roughly 20% and 80%. You don't need to be obsessive:

- Top up when you can rather than waiting for single digits.
- If your phone has **Protect battery / Limit to 85%** (Settings → Battery), it's fine to enable it for longevity.
- Overnight charging is safe on modern phones (they stop at 100%), so don't lose sleep over it.

## Common battery myths to ignore

- "Close all apps to save battery" — closing apps you'll reopen uses *more* energy than leaving them in memory. Only force-stop the ones that misbehave.
- "Battery-saver apps extend battery life" — they don't, and many are adware.
- "Charge to 100% then drain to 0% to recalibrate" — unnecessary on modern phones.

## When to replace the battery

If your phone dies above 30%, shuts off randomly, or shows battery health below ~80%, the battery itself is worn. A genuine replacement is usually far cheaper than a new phone — and often restores a full day of use.

> Tip: If your phone is *also* running slowly, battery wear and general sluggishness often go together. Work through our [guide to fixing a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) for the full picture.

## Wrapping up

Start with battery usage in Settings to find the real drainers, then enable Battery Saver, tame your screen, and restrict background apps. Those four steps alone will get most people through the day — no paid apps required.
