AI photo editing used to mean expensive software and a learning curve. These days, many of the most useful edits — removing a background, sharpening a blurry shot, cleaning up an old family photo — take seconds and are either free or built into apps you already have.

Here's what AI editors actually do under the hood, plus step-by-step workflows for the tasks people search for most.

## What an AI photo editor actually does

Traditional editors work pixel by pixel — you select an area, and the software applies a filter. AI editors do something different: they've been trained on millions of photos, and they *recognize what's in your image*. That's how they can:

- **Understand objects.** It knows the person in the foreground is separate from the background, so it can cut them out.
- **Fill in missing detail.** Remove an object, and it paints over the hole using patterns from the rest of the photo.
- **Generate what should be there.** Upscale a blurry face, and it adds realistic detail that wasn't in the original — detail it *imagined* from its training.

That last point matters: AI edits are an interpretation, not a faithful copy. Faces can change, text can garble, and fine detail can shift. Always check the result at full zoom.

## What you'll need

- The photo or photos you want to edit
- A web browser or one of the free apps mentioned below (the web tools need nothing to install)
- Patience for one or two attempts — AI tools rarely nail it perfectly on the first try

## How to remove a background with AI

1. Open the photo in a background-removal tool. Good free options: **remove.bg** (web, no signup for quick use) or the **Canva** background remover (free tier).
2. Upload your image.
3. Wait a few seconds — the AI detects the subject and deletes the background automatically.
4. Refine if needed. Most tools let you paint over areas the AI missed or erase bits it kept.
5. Download the result as a PNG (PNG keeps the transparent background; JPEG doesn't).
6. Drop that cut-out onto any new background in Canva, PowerPoint, or even a phone app like Google Photos.

## How to fix a blurry photo with AI

Realistically, AI can't resurrect a completely out-of-focus photo — there's no detail to recover. But it can sharpen a slightly soft photo and improve a low-resolution one.

1. Use a dedicated upscaler. **Upscayl** is a free, open-source option for desktop (Windows, Mac, Linux) that runs locally, so your photos never leave your computer. Adobe Photoshop's "Super Resolution" does the same thing inside Photoshop.
2. Upload the blurry image and pick an upscale factor (2x or 4x are typical).
3. Compare before and after at full zoom. If faces or text look warped, try a lower factor or a different model.
4. Save the result. You may need to sharpen it again lightly after downscaling.

For the built-in option: **Google Photos** includes a "photo unblur" tool on supported Pixel phones that improves faces and detail in slightly blurry shots.

## How to enhance old or damaged photos

Old photos are where AI genuinely shines — removing scratches, evening out faded color, and sharpening faces.

1. Scan the photo at the highest resolution your scanner or phone will manage.
2. Open it in an enhancement tool. **Google Photos** (its enhancement and eraser tools), **Canva**, and **Adobe Photoshop** (the "Neural Filters" panel) all handle this well.
3. Apply enhancements one step at a time: first fix color and exposure, then remove damage, then sharpen. Doing it all at once tends to over-process.
4. Zoom in and check edges — AI can "invent" detail on faces, so make sure the person still looks like the person.
5. Export a high-resolution copy and keep the original scan saved separately.

## How to remove objects or people from a photo

1. Open the image in **Google Photos** on a supported phone and tap **Edit**, then the object eraser tool (it may be called "Magic Eraser," "Magic Editor," or "object eraser" depending on your phone).
2. Circle or tap the object you want gone.
3. The AI analyzes the surrounding pixels and fills the gap.
4. If the first attempt looks off, tap it again or select a smaller area. Keep the edit subtle — removing something cleanly is hard when the background behind it is busy.

## How to retouch a portrait

Built-in portrait AI is everywhere in 2026:

- **Google Photos** and **Samsung Gallery** offer automatic subject enhancement and face retouching.
- **Canva's** photo editor can smooth skin and whiten teeth with sliders (use them lightly).
- **Adobe Photoshop's** generative tools can remove blemishes and even reposition eyes or change expressions.

A rule worth keeping: retouching portraits is about small adjustments. Heavy AI smoothing makes faces look waxy and unnatural. Zoom to 100% and check the eyes and hairline before exporting.

## Privacy notes

Two things to remember when editing photos with AI. First, most web-based editors upload your photo to a server, and some use uploads to improve their models — check the tool's privacy policy before putting sensitive photos through it. Second, if that matters to you, local tools like **Upscayl** or [running AI on your own computer](/articles/run-ai-on-your-computer-local-ai) keep everything on-device. And before you start piling up big photo libraries, it's worth a refresher on [freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone).

AI editors have turned photo editing from a skill into a series of short steps: upload, click, refine, download. Remove backgrounds in seconds, sharpen soft shots, and rescue old family photos — just remember the AI is guessing, check your results closely, and keep your originals backed up.
