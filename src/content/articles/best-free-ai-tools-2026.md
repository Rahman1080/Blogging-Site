You don't need a paid subscription to get real value out of AI. The big players all offer genuinely useful free tiers, and a handful of free tools are good enough that many people never need to pay.

One caveat before the list: free tiers change. Limits shrink, features move behind paywalls, and "free" sometimes means "free for now." Treat this list as a starting point, check the current pricing page on each tool, and use the free version before you commit to anything.

## Writing and text

**ChatGPT (free tier)** — the most famous chatbot for a reason. The free tier handles drafting emails, rewriting, summarizing, and brainstorming. Pair it with the prompt formula in our [beginner guide to writing ChatGPT prompts](/articles/how-to-write-better-chatgpt-prompts).

**Google Gemini** — Google's chatbot, with a free tier. It ties into Google's ecosystem, which makes it handy for summarizing and drafting from documents you already keep in Google Drive.

**Claude** — known for long-form writing and a natural, thoughtful tone. The free tier gives you a daily allowance of messages, which is great for editing and rewriting drafts.

**Microsoft Copilot** — free tier, built into Windows and Edge. It's on most PCs already, and it's fine for everyday drafting.

**DeepL** — a translation tool that reads far more naturally than older machine translators. The free tier translates a generous amount of text per day, and there's a browser extension.

**Grammarly** — the free version catches typos and basic grammar while you type in your browser and many desktop apps. The paid tier adds style suggestions, but free is genuinely useful.

## Images and photo editing

**Canva** — the free tier includes an AI background remover, AI image generation, and one-click resizing. For non-designers it's the easiest place to start. See [how to edit photos with AI](/articles/how-to-edit-photos-with-ai) for step-by-step workflows.

**remove.bg** — does exactly one thing: removes image backgrounds in seconds, free on smaller images. No signup needed for quick single uses.

**Google Photos** — includes free built-in editing tricks like an object eraser on supported phones, plus automatic photo enhancement.

**Photopea** — a free browser-based editor that feels like Photoshop. It includes AI-assisted selection tools and works without installing anything.

## Audio and voice

**ElevenLabs** — the free tier gives you a monthly allowance of AI voice generation — enough to test narration for videos or short content.

**Audacity** — free, open-source audio editor. Not AI itself, but it pairs well with free AI voice tools for cleaning up recordings.

**Otter.ai** — the free tier transcribes meetings and voice notes automatically. Useful for turning recordings into searchable text.

## Study and learning

**Perplexity** — an AI search engine that answers questions with citations. Because it shows sources, it's one of the better free tools for research. (Keep the habit of checking those sources — even cited answers can be wrong.)

**Your phone's built-in assistant** — Google's assistant with Gemini and Siri can both explain topics, summarize text, and help with homework, free and already installed.

**Notion AI** — note-taking with built-in AI for summarizing and organizing. The free personal tier includes a limited AI allowance.

## Productivity

**ChatGPT and Gemini** top the list for everyday productivity: turning messy notes into a plan, drafting a tricky email, or summarizing a long document. If you're new to what these models actually are and how they work, start with [what AI is](/articles/what-is-ai-explained).

## What's genuinely free vs "free to start"

Here's the honest version of how free tiers work in 2026:

- **Usage caps are the norm.** Most free tiers limit you to a set number of messages, generations, or minutes per day or month. Hitting a cap usually means waiting or paying.
- **Feature gates are common.** The newest model, voice mode, or higher-resolution image generation often sits behind a subscription.
- **Watermarks and limits on images and video.** Free image and video generators typically add watermarks or cap resolution.
- **Your data may be used for training.** Free tiers of consumer AI tools often train on your conversations. If that bothers you, see [what happens to the data you give AI chatbots](/articles/ai-chatbot-privacy-data).

That "free to start" pattern is why you should read the pricing page before building a whole workflow around a tool. A tool you can use free for a week is useful; a tool that yanks away its free tier mid-project is a headache. When a free version is enough for your actual volume of work, it's genuinely free — most people simply don't hit the caps on everyday use.

## How to pick the right free tool

1. Start with the category you actually need — writing, images, audio, study, or productivity.
2. Try the free tier of the two or three biggest names in that category.
3. Compare them on one real task you do regularly, not on hype.
4. Only upgrade if the free version clearly isn't enough.
