Clearing your browser's cookies and cache is the fix-it trick that solves more everyday problems than almost anything else — pages that won't load, sites showing old versions of themselves, and a browser that's started feeling slow. It takes about a minute in any browser, and you can't really break anything.

Before you do it, here's the short version of what you're clearing and what it'll cost you. Then I'll walk through Chrome, Safari, and Firefox step by step.

## What cookies and cache actually do

Your browser keeps two kinds of memory about the sites you visit.

**The cache** is a stash of page pieces — images, logos, style sheets — stored locally so sites load faster the second time. When it gets stale, you see old versions of pages or pages that break.

**Cookies** are small files that remember you: your login, your preferences, your cart, your language choice. Without them, every site would treat you like a brand-new visitor every single visit.

Neither one is secret. Cookies aren't "spying files," and clearing them won't fix your privacy in any meaningful way. That said, the sites you *don't* want following you around are a separate conversation — and knowing how to [spot a phishing email](/articles/how-to-spot-phishing-email) matters far more for your security than wiping your cache.

## What you lose when you clear them

Be clear-eyed about the trade before you click.

- **Logins.** On most sites you'll be signed out and have to log in again.
- **Preferences.** Theme choices, country settings, and "accept cookies" prompts may come back.
- **Cached speed.** Pages will reload slowly once, then speed back up as the cache refills.

You will **not** lose your bookmarks, your history (in most browsers), your saved passwords, or your installed extensions. Those are stored in separate files.

## How to clear cache and cookies in Chrome

Google Chrome on Windows and Mac works the same way:

1. Open Chrome and click the **three-dot menu** (top right).
2. Go to **More tools → Clear browsing data**.
3. Pick a time range from the dropdown. **All time** clears everything; a shorter range only clears recent stuff.
4. Tick the boxes for **Browsing history**, **Cookies and other site data**, and **Cached images and files**.
5. Click **Clear data**.

If you only want cookies (not history), untick **Browsing history** and leave the other two checked. Chrome lets you be precise, which is nice when you want to keep your recent history but nuke everything else.

## How to clear cache and cookies in Safari

On a Mac:

1. Open Safari and go to **Safari → Settings** (or **Preferences** on older macOS).
2. Click the **Privacy** tab.
3. Click **Manage Website Data…** to remove cookies for specific sites, or **Remove All Website Data** to clear everything.
4. Confirm in the pop-up.

That removes cookies and stored data. Safari's cache hides in the same bucket, so you're usually done here. If a page still misbehaves, the nuclear option is **Develop → Empty Caches** — but only after you turn on the Develop menu (**Settings → Advanced → Show features for web developers**).

## How to clear cache and cookies in Firefox

1. Open Firefox and click the **hamburger menu** (three horizontal lines, top right).
2. Go to **Settings → Privacy & Security**.
3. Scroll to **Cookies and Site Data** and click **Clear Data…**.
4. Tick **Cookies and Site Data** and **Cached Web Content**, then click **Clear**.
5. Firefox will ask you to confirm — click **Clear** again.

Firefox also lets you clear everything (including history) from the same menu via **Clear Data** or by pressing the shortcut that opens its clear-history window, which is handy when you want a full sweep.

## Clear cookies for just one site

Clearing *everything* is overkill when only one site is misbehaving — say, a store page that keeps showing an empty cart, or a news site stuck on yesterday. Every browser can target a single site:

- **Chrome:** click the **lock icon** (or "Site settings") in the address bar → **Cookies and site data** → **Manage cookies and site data** → remove the site.
- **Firefox:** click the **shield icon** in the address bar → **Clear cookies and site data** for that site only.
- **Safari:** **Safari → Settings → Privacy → Manage Website Data…**, then select the site and click **Remove**.

This keeps your logins everywhere else intact while fixing just the problem site. It's the surgical option most people don't know exists.

## What about mobile browsers?

The same clearing exists on phones, tucked into each browser's settings. On Chrome for Android: **three-dot menu → Settings → Privacy and security → Clear browsing data**. On Safari on iPhone: **Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data**. The mobile options are coarser — usually "clear everything" rather than fine-grained choices — which is fine for the one-time fix you're doing.

## Do you need to do this often?

No. The honest answer is that most people never need to clear their cache. Browsers manage it automatically, and clearing it constantly just makes the web slower for you while it refills. Clear it when something breaks:

- A page shows old content or won't load no matter how many times you refresh.
- A site's layout looks broken or images won't appear.
- You want to force yourself signed out everywhere, fast.

If your browser feels sluggish in general, clearing the cache is a reasonable one-off step — but it's not a maintenance habit. For a genuinely slow machine, the fixes in our [guide to speeding up Windows 11](/articles/speed-up-windows-11-pc) go a lot further.

## Wrapping up

Clearing cookies and cache is a safe, two-minute fix for a handful of common browser problems. You'll lose your logins and a bit of speed on the next page load; you won't lose your passwords or bookmarks. And you don't need to schedule it — just reach for it the next time a site acts up.
