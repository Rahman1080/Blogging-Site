You don't need a camera, a studio, or editing experience to make a video anymore. With AI tools, you can go from a rough idea to a finished, narrated video in an afternoon — most of it free.

Here's the honest beginner workflow, step by step, plus what AI video tools are actually good at and where they'll frustrate you.

## What AI video tools can and can't do

**Good at:** turning text into short clips (often 5-15 seconds per clip), creating simple animations and "faceless" videos, generating voiceovers, adding captions automatically, and assembling cutaways for talking-head videos.

**Not good at:** long, complex, consistent scenes; realistic people (hands and faces still go weird); fine control over exactly what happens on screen; and anything with a long story arc. Most tools add a watermark on free tiers.

Realistic expectation: AI is a fast way to make *simple* videos. For polished, complicated productions, it's a helper, not a replacement.

## What you'll need

- A script, or at least a clear idea (AI can help write this too)
- A free account on one or two AI tools (see below)
- A computer or phone — most tools work in the browser

## Step-by-step: from idea to finished video

### 1. Write the script

Every good video starts with a script. Keep it short — for your first video, aim for one idea in about 200-300 words. Write it conversationally, the way you'd talk. A chatbot is great for this: "Turn these bullet points into a 250-word script for a voiceover about [topic]: [your points]." The [prompt guide](/articles/how-to-write-better-chatgpt-prompts) explains how to get the script you actually want.

### 2. Generate the voiceover

Upload your script to a text-to-speech tool. Options with free tiers include **ElevenLabs**, **Canva's** AI voiceover, and the voices built into most video tools. Listen carefully and pick a voice that fits — natural voices sound far better than obviously robotic ones. If you'd rather use your own voice, record it on your phone and skip this step.

### 3. Generate the visuals

This is where your choices matter most. You have a few routes:

- **Text-to-video.** Paste a description into a video generator (like **Runway**, **Pika**, **Luma Dream Machine**, or **Canva's** video tools) and it generates a short clip from your words. Keep prompts simple and visual: "a sunrise over a quiet beach, soft colors."
- **AI images with motion.** Generate images with an image tool, then add subtle zoom or pan in an editor. This is cheaper and often looks better than raw text-to-video.
- **Stock footage.** Many free stock sites let you download clips to match your script, skipping the generation entirely.

A good practical move: combine a few generated clips with stock footage instead of generating everything.

### 4. Assemble and edit

Put your clips in order in an editor. **CapCut** (free tier, web and mobile), **Canva's** video editor, or the free tier of **DaVinci Resolve** all work. Drop the voiceover on the timeline, line up the clips, and add captions — most of these tools auto-generate captions from your audio with AI, which improves watchability a lot.

### 5. Add captions and polish

Auto-caption the whole video, fix any misheard words, and add a simple title card. Keep transitions simple (cuts are fine). Check the pacing: if the visuals and voiceover feel mismatched, trim or extend clips.

### 6. Export and review

Export at the highest quality your free tier allows, then watch the whole thing once through with fresh eyes. Check the captions, the audio levels, and whether any AI-generated clip looks obviously off. Fix and re-export if needed.

## Choosing your tools (honestly)

There are dozens of AI video tools now, and free tiers change constantly. Rather than chase the latest one, pick based on your goal:

- **Faceless narration videos** (history, top-10, explainer style): script + voiceover + stock footage + captions is the fastest, most reliable route.
- **Short social clips:** CapCut's free tier packs more AI features into one place than most paid tools.
- **Experimental or artistic clips:** try dedicated text-to-video generators — but expect to generate many takes to get one good one.

Whichever you pick, check the free tier's watermark policy and export limits *before* you invest hours.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Writing a script that's too long.** A 250-word script is a much better first video than a 2,000-word one.
- **Expecting one take to be perfect.** AI generation is a numbers game. Generate multiple options, then pick the good one.
- **Ignoring the audio.** A great voiceover with average visuals beats great visuals with a bad voiceover.
- **Forgetting the watermark.** If you're posting publicly, plan around watermarks or budget for a paid tier.
