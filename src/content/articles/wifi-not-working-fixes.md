Unreliable Wi-Fi is one of the most common — and most fixable — home tech frustrations. Before you call your internet provider or buy new gear, work through these fixes in order. Most people solve the problem within the first half.

## 1. Restart your router and modem

The classic fix works for a reason: routers accumulate stale connections and memory bloat over time.

1. Unplug the router (and modem, if separate) from power.
2. Wait 30 seconds.
3. Plug them back in and wait 2 minutes for them to fully start.
4. Reconnect your devices and test.

If your Wi-Fi has been flaky for days, do this first — it resolves a large share of issues instantly.

## 2. Restart the device that's having trouble

If one device keeps dropping while others work, the problem is on that device:

- **Phone/tablet:** toggle Wi-Fi off and on, or restart the device.
- **Laptop:** restart, and check for driver updates in Device Manager (Windows) or Software Update (Mac).
- Turn off Wi-Fi **power saving** if your laptop's settings offer it (some laptops aggressively drop Wi-Fi to save battery).

## 3. Check whether it's your internet or your Wi-Fi

Run this test to know where the fault lies:

- Connect a computer to the router **with an Ethernet cable**.
- If the wired connection is fast and stable, the problem is your Wi-Fi network (keep reading).
- If the wired connection is also slow, the issue is your internet connection or provider — contact them.

## 4. Move your router to a better spot

Placement is everything. The router should be:

- **Central** in your home, not in a corner cabinet.
- **High up** — on a shelf, not on the floor.
- **Away** from walls, metal objects, fish tanks, microwaves, and cordless phones.
- **Not hidden** behind a TV or in a closet.

Even a 3-foot move can transform coverage.

## 5. Change the Wi-Fi channel (2.4 GHz)

Neighbor networks on the same channel cause congestion.

1. Log into your router's admin page (usually `192.168.0.1` or `192.168.1.1` — check the sticker on the router).
2. Find the **Wireless / Wi-Fi settings**.
3. On 2.4 GHz, set the channel to **1, 6, or 11** — try whichever is least crowded (use a free Wi-Fi analyzer app to check).

On 5 GHz, "Auto" channel selection usually works well; changing channels is less necessary there.

## 6. Use the 5 GHz band for speed

- Modern routers broadcast two networks: **2.4 GHz** (long range, slower) and **5 GHz** (shorter range, faster).
- For devices near the router (streaming, gaming, video calls), connect to the 5 GHz network.
- Keep IoT devices and things far from the router on 2.4 GHz.

## 7. Update your router firmware

Router manufacturers ship bug fixes and security patches as firmware updates.

1. Log into the admin page.
2. Look for **Firmware Update / Administration → Update**.
3. Install any update, then restart the router.

This is also a security step — an unpatched router is a common entry point for home-network attacks.

## 8. Reduce interference

Microwaves, cordless phones, baby monitors, and many Bluetooth devices share the 2.4 GHz band. If Wi-Fi drops when the microwave runs, that's interference. Moving the router away, or shifting important devices to 5 GHz, usually fixes it.

## 9. Change your Wi-Fi security settings

Old encryption standards (WEP, WPA) are both slow and insecure. In your router's wireless settings, make sure security is set to **WPA2 or WPA3**. This also prevents neighbors from leeching bandwidth.

## 10. Prioritize important devices (QoS)

If video calls or gaming suffer when others stream:

- In router settings, find **QoS (Quality of Service)**.
- Prioritize the devices you care about (work laptop, TV, gaming console).

## 11. Consider mesh or an upgrade

If your home is large, thick-walled, or multi-story, a single router may just not be enough:

- **Mesh systems** (e.g. popular Wi-Fi 6 mesh kits) give whole-home coverage with one network name and smooth roaming.
- **A newer router** with Wi-Fi 6 is the highest-impact hardware upgrade if your current one is 5+ years old.
- Try this only after the software fixes above — new gear can't fix a placement or channel problem.

## 12. Factory reset the router (last resort)

A reset clears all custom settings back to defaults — you'll need to set up your network name and password again.

1. Find the small reset pinhole on the router.
2. Hold a paperclip in it for ~10 seconds until lights blink.
3. Reconfigure the router from the admin page.

## Wrapping up

Restart → reposition → change the channel → update firmware. That sequence resolves the large majority of home Wi-Fi problems without spending a cent. If your internet is genuinely fast on a wired connection and the router is newer, focus on placement and channel.
