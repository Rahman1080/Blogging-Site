# How to Use Your Phone as a Mobile Hotspot

When the hotel Wi-Fi fails, the cafe's network demands a phone number, or you're working from a park bench, your phone is already an internet source. Turning it into a mobile hotspot lets your laptop, tablet, or friend's device borrow that connection over Wi-Fi — no router required.

It takes about thirty seconds to set up on either platform. Here's how to share your phone's internet on Android and iPhone, secure it properly, and avoid the two mistakes people make (burning their data and running a wide-open hotspot).

## What a hotspot actually does

A mobile hotspot turns your phone into a mini Wi-Fi router. It takes the phone's cellular connection — LTE or 5G — and rebroadcasts it as a Wi-Fi network that other devices can join. Any device with Wi-Fi can connect: laptops, tablets, game consoles, other phones.

There's one thing to understand before you start: everything those devices do flows through your mobile data plan. The hotspot doesn't create internet; it spends yours.

## Turn on the hotspot on Android

The steps are similar across phone brands, with slightly different menu names.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Network & internet → Hotspot & tethering** (some phones call it **Tethering & portable hotspot** or put it under **Connections**).
3. Tap **Wi-Fi hotspot** (or **Portable Wi-Fi hotspot**).
4. Toggle it **on**.

The phone will show you the network name and password. Note the password before you connect — most phones let you see or edit it by tapping the hotspot settings. On many Androids you can also set a **Max connections** limit and, importantly, choose **Auto-off** so the hotspot doesn't run forever in your pocket.

## Turn on the hotspot on iPhone

Apple calls it **Personal Hotspot**, and it's slightly more tucked away.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Personal Hotspot**.
3. Turn on **Allow Others to Join**.

You'll see the network name and a password you can edit. One nice iPhone detail: if your other devices are signed into the same Apple ID, they can often join the hotspot automatically without you entering the password — a toggle called **Instant Hotspot** handles that.

## Connect a laptop or tablet

Connecting is the same as joining any Wi-Fi network.

1. On the device, open its Wi-Fi settings.
2. Find your phone's network name in the list.
3. Select it and enter the hotspot password.

Once connected, the device has internet access. The phone will show a small indicator that a device is tethered, and you'll usually see how many devices are connected.

## Other ways to share the connection

Wi-Fi hotspot is the best-known method, but it's not the only one — and for certain situations, the alternatives are better.

**USB tethering** shares your phone's internet through a USB cable. It's the most stable option: no Wi-Fi interference, no battery-draining radio, and the phone charges while it works. On Android, find it in the same **Hotspot & tethering** menu and enable **USB tethering**. On iPhone, plug the phone into the computer, and the Personal Hotspot settings offer the USB option when a cable is connected. It's the right choice when you're at a desk and want a rock-solid connection.

**Bluetooth tethering** is a slower fallback that barely touches battery. Both platforms offer it in the same settings screen. It's fine for quick email or messaging but too slow for streaming or big downloads, so treat it as the emergency option.

None of these cost anything extra — they all use the same mobile data. The difference is purely in how the connection gets from your phone to the other device.

## Secure the hotspot (don't skip this)

An open hotspot is an invitation. Anyone within range can join, and worse, traffic through an unsecured hotspot can be intercepted. Both platforms lock this down by default, but it's worth confirming:

- **Use a strong password.** Change the default if it looks weak. Open the hotspot settings and set a password you don't use anywhere else.
- **Check the security type.** You want **WPA2** or **WPA3** (older WEP is insecure). Modern phones use WPA2 or WPA3 automatically.
- **Turn it off when you're done.** A hotspot left running drains battery and keeps broadcasting your network name. Both platforms offer an auto-off timer or a "hide my hotspot" option — use them.

## The honest notes on speed and data

Hotspot speeds depend on your phone's signal, not the phone itself. If the phone shows one bar, the hotspot will feel like dial-up no matter how new the device is. Moving the phone near a window, or holding it higher, often helps more than any setting. Speeds are also shared: three devices connected means each one splits the same connection.

Battery life is the other quiet cost. Running a hotspot keeps the phone's cellular radio and Wi-Fi radio active at the same time, which drains the battery noticeably faster than normal use. For long sessions, keep the phone plugged into a charger — a hotspot running on low battery can shut down mid-download without warning.

And the data warning is worth repeating: streaming a movie to your laptop over the hotspot can chew through a month of data in an evening. If your plan has a separate hotspot allowance, check it before long sessions. For stretching what you've got, our guide to [reducing mobile data usage](/articles/how-to-reduce-mobile-data) covers the settings that keep connected devices from eating everything.

If the hotspot is on but devices still can't get online, the problem is often the phone's own connection rather than the hotspot. The usual fixes — restart the phone, toggle the hotspot off and on, check for a mobile data outage — are covered in our guide to [Wi-Fi dropping and slow connections](/articles/wifi-not-working-fixes).

## Wrapping up

Your phone is a portable router whenever you need one. Turn on the hotspot on Android or Personal Hotspot on iPhone, connect your devices with a strong password, and remember that everything streams through your data plan. Set a strong password, keep an eye on usage, and turn it off when you're finished.
