An iPhone battery that drains fast is rarely one thing — it's a handful of habits and settings stacked on top of each other. Work through these in order, from the quickest wins to the one fix you can't download: a replacement battery.

Before changing anything, look at the evidence. **Settings → Battery** shows a list of apps and how much battery each used in the last 24 hours or 10 days. Whatever's on top is where the power is going. Use that list as your guide.

## 1. Check your screen brightness and screen-on time

The screen is the single biggest battery consumer on any phone, and the battery screen tells you how much time you've spent looking at it.

- Turn on **Auto-Brightness** (Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Auto-Brightness) so the phone dims itself.
- Pull down Control Center and drag the brightness slider down when you're indoors.
- Consider **Dark Mode** (Settings → Display & Brightness) — it helps on OLED iPhones.

If the battery list shows "On Screen" times in the many hours, you're not looking at a fault; you're looking at heavy use, and nothing will fix that except using the phone less.

## 2. Stop apps from refreshing in the background

Background App Refresh lets apps fetch new content before you open them. Convenient, but it's a slow battery drain — especially for apps that poll constantly.

1. Go to **Settings → General → Background App Refresh**.
2. Set it to **Off**, or **Wi-Fi** at most, or scroll the list and switch it off for the apps that don't need to be up to date (your weather app can wait until you open it).

You can also leave Background App Refresh alone and just use **Low Power Mode** — it pauses the same background work automatically.

## 3. Deal with poor signal

This is the sneakiest drain of all. When your phone is in an area with weak cellular signal, it cranks up its radios to hold the connection, and that eats battery fast. If your battery list shows **"No Cell Coverage"** or lots of **"Low Signal"** time, the phone has been grinding away just staying connected.

- Switch on **Wi-Fi Calling** if your carrier offers it, so the phone uses Wi-Fi instead of a weak tower.
- In a dead zone, enable **Airplane Mode** — better than letting the phone burn battery hunting for a signal.
- Turn off **5G** if you don't need it: **Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Options → Voice & Data**, and pick LTE. 5G is faster but hungrier.

## 4. Tame notifications and location services

- **Notifications:** every buzz wakes the screen. Go to **Settings → Notifications**, and switch off anything that doesn't genuinely need your attention. You're not losing anything important by silencing the games and shopping apps.
- **Location:** **Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services** lists every app that's used your location. Change any app you don't need tracking you from "Always" to "While Using." Apps that run in the background for location (weather, delivery trackers) are quiet drains.

## 5. Reboot the phone occasionally

iPhones rarely need restarts, but a phone that's been on for weeks accumulates background junk and background processes that quietly chew power. A restart — hold the side button and one of the volume buttons, then slide to power off — clears it all. It's free, it takes a minute, and it's a legitimate fix for a battery that's suddenly worse.

## 6. Give a recent iOS update a few days

Install a big iOS update and your battery may be worse for a day or two while the phone reindexes photos, optimizes storage, and runs background maintenance. This usually settles within a few days. If you upgraded very recently, that alone might explain the "sudden" drain — give it a moment before you start changing settings.

## 7. Check the battery health — honestly

Some drain can't be fixed with settings, and it's important to be straight about it. A lithium battery degrades with every charge cycle, and a few-year-old iPhone simply doesn't hold as much as it did on day one.

1. Open **Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging**.
2. Look at **Maximum Capacity**. Apple rates a battery as needing service at around **80% or below**.

If you're at 85% and the phone dies fast, settings tweaks still help at the margins, but you're fighting physics. If you're at 78% with sudden shutdowns, **the battery needs replacing** — no app, setting, or "battery fix" tool will restore the runtime. Apple can replace it in-store or by mail, and it's usually the most effective speed and battery upgrade an older iPhone can get.

## 8. Use Low Power Mode as a tool, not a lifestyle

Low Power Mode genuinely helps — it pauses background refresh, reduces visual effects, and trims performance. The honest caveat: it's a band-aid for the last 20% of a charge, not a cure for a drained phone.

Turn it on from **Control Center** (tap the battery icon) or **Settings → Battery**, or set it to kick in automatically at a chosen percentage.

## Wrapping up

Start with the evidence in Settings → Battery, then work the list: screen, background refresh, signal, notifications, location. Restart, give updates a few days, and be honest about battery health — a battery at 80% or below is a hardware problem with a hardware fix.
