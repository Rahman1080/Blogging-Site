Every tech problem you'll ever face is one of a handful of recurring ones. Slow device. Wi-Fi won't connect. Storage full. App frozen. No sound. The fixes are short, mostly free, and usually start with the same step: restart it.

Think of this as the index to every troubleshooting article on the site. Each problem below gets the quick fix and a link to the full guide when you need to dig deeper.

## The restart-first rule

The most powerful troubleshooting tool you own is the off switch. A restart clears stuck apps, hung services, half-connected devices, and a thousand tiny states that accumulate while a computer runs. It costs a minute and can't hurt anything.

So make it the default move: **before you Google a problem, restart the thing.** It won't fix every issue, but it'll fix more of them than any single other step — and it's free.

## Slow computer or phone

Symptoms: everything takes twice as long, spinning cursors, apps that feel heavy. The usual causes, in order:

1. **Too much running at once** — close what you're not using.
2. **Storage nearly full** — a full drive slows everything down. Clear space or move files off.
3. **Background updates** — updates install in the background and eat CPU while you work.
4. **An aging device** — after a few years, even a healthy device can feel slow, and updates stop helping.

A restart and a storage check handles most cases. When it's a deeper problem, our [Windows 11 speed-up guide](/articles/speed-up-windows-11-pc) and [fix for a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) walk through the full lists.

## Wi-Fi won't connect or keeps dropping

Symptoms: no internet, or a connection that works for a minute then dies. Quick ladder:

1. **Restart the router** — unplug it for a minute, plug it back in, wait for it to fully start.
2. **Forget and rejoin the network** on the affected device.
3. **Check other devices** — if everything fails, it's the router; if just one device, it's the device.
4. **Move closer** — walls and distance kill Wi-Fi fast.

If it's still flaky after that, our [Wi-Fi troubleshooting guide](/articles/wifi-not-working-fixes) covers channels, placement, and interference in detail.

## Storage is full

Symptoms: "Storage almost full" warnings, a device that suddenly won't update or install apps. The fastest fixes:

1. **Check what's using the space** — your phone or computer's storage settings show the breakdown.
2. **Clear the trash or recently deleted folders** — deleted files sit there for weeks, still eating space.
3. **Offload photos and videos** — move them to the cloud or a computer instead of deleting them.
4. **Clear app caches** — temporary files that pile up quietly.

That's the short version; our [phone storage guide](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) has the full method for Android and iPhone.

## An app freezes or crashes

Symptoms: a spinning beach ball, a "not responding" window, an app that closes itself. The fix:

1. **Force-quit the app** — Task Manager on Windows (Ctrl+Shift+Esc → End task), Force Quit on Mac (Option+Cmd+Esc), or the app switcher on a phone.
2. **Reopen it** — most freezes were a one-time glitch.
3. **Update the app** — crashes often get fixed in updates.
4. **If it keeps crashing**, clear its cache, or uninstall and reinstall.

App-specific crash loops usually have a version-specific cause, so search the app's name plus "crashing" for the current fix.

## No sound

Symptoms: videos play silently, apps act normal, nothing comes out. Quick ladder:

1. **Check mute and volume** — the system, the app, and physical speakers all have their own controls.
2. **Check the output device** — audio may be going to Bluetooth headphones that aren't connected.
3. **Restart the audio service** (Windows) or restart the app (Mac).
4. **Restart the computer.**

The full walkthrough — including the volume mixer and driver fixes — is in our [no-sound-on-computer guide](/articles/no-sound-on-computer).

## Two more classics: battery and won't turn on

Two problems round out the common list, and both have fast first-aid:

**Battery draining fast.** Start with the screen brightness and the apps doing work in the background. A restart clears anything stuck. Then check which app is the top battery user in your device's settings — it's usually an app running in the background or an old device. Not charging or charging slowly? Try a different cable and charger before anything else, since cables are the part that wears out.

**Device won't turn on.** Before you panic, check the obvious: is it charged (and does the charger actually work)? Is the screen on but black (try the brightness and a restart)? Try holding the power button for ten to fifteen seconds to force a restart. If it's been plugged in, charging, and still nothing after a few minutes, it may be a hardware issue worth professional help.

## Something still won't work? Try this order

When a device misbehaves in a way that doesn't fit the list above, run this order and stop when one step fixes it:

1. **Restart the device.** The universal first step.
2. **Restart the network gear** (router, modem) if it's a connection problem.
3. **Update the software** — the OS and the app both.
4. **Clear the cache** for the affected app or browser.
5. **Check the manufacturer's support site** for your exact model and error message.
6. **Reset the device to defaults** — the last resort before calling it a hardware problem.

That sequence covers the vast majority of everyday tech problems. The key habit is doing the cheap steps first instead of jumping to drastic ones.

## Wrapping up

Almost every tech problem is one of a small handful, and they all respond to the same discipline: restart first, check the simple causes, and only escalate when the quick fixes fail.
