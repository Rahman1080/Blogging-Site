You delete a hundred photos, check the storage screen, and the bar has barely moved. It feels like the phone is ignoring you. It isn't — but the space you think you freed is being held somewhere else.

Here's what's actually going on and exactly how to claw the space back.

## Why the storage bar doesn't move

Deleting photos doesn't free space instantly for three reasons, and usually all three are at work at once.

### 1. Recently Deleted holds your photos for 30 days

This is the big one. When you delete photos in the Photos app, they don't leave your phone — they move to the **Recently Deleted** album, where they sit for 30 days before the phone actually wipes them. Until then, they take up exactly the same space.

If you "deleted" hundreds of photos last week, the space is still fully occupied.

### 2. Messages attachments are separate from your photo library

Photos and videos you send or receive in text threads are stored in the **Messages app**, not in Photos. So when you clean out your Photo Library, the gigabytes sitting in conversations are completely untouched.

Long chats with heavy media are the single most overlooked source of storage on a busy iPhone.

### 3. System Data and cached files fill the gaps

Scroll to the bottom of **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** and you'll see a slice labeled **System Data** (it's shown as "Other" on some iOS versions). This bucket holds caches, logs, temporary files, and app junk. It grows on its own, especially after you install updates or use media-heavy apps, and deleting photos does nothing to it.

On top of that, a phone that's been running low on space gets stuck: iOS needs free room to do housekeeping, so the caches keep piling up in a loop.

## Step 1: Empty Recently Deleted properly

1. Open **Photos**.
2. Tap **Albums**, scroll to the bottom, and tap **Recently Deleted**.
3. Tap **Select** in the top-right, then **Delete All** at the bottom. Confirm.
4. Also check **Recently Deleted** in the **Files** app (on newer iOS versions) for good measure.

That one step reclaims every photo and video you thought you'd deleted in the last month. It's almost always the fastest way to free a meaningful chunk of space.

## Step 2: See what your storage screen actually says

Go to **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**. Wait a moment for the bar chart to load. Read it honestly:

- **Photos** at the top means your Photo Library (minus trash) is still big.
- **Messages** means conversations are eating space — tap it to see your heaviest chats listed at the top.
- **System Data** (or Other) means caches and app junk — the fix is below.
- The list of apps shows the biggest offenders; tap any app for a size breakdown.

Whatever the biggest category is, that's your real target.

## Step 3: Turn on Optimize iPhone Storage

If you use iCloud Photos, there's a setting that frees space automatically — and most people don't realize they're on the "keep everything" setting.

1. Open **Settings → Photos**.
2. Under "iCloud Photos," choose **Optimize iPhone Storage** instead of "Download and Keep Originals."

With optimization on, iOS keeps smaller versions of photos on the phone and stores full-resolution originals in iCloud. Your photos stay viewable (you can download any original on demand over Wi-Fi), but the phone drops the full-size files. On a large library this can free several gigabytes instantly.

The honest trade-off: offline, an optimized photo is lower resolution until you download it. If you're about to travel somewhere without signal, you might prefer originals for a while. And optimization only works if your photos are actually backed up — see the [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) to confirm yours are.

## Step 4: Trim the hidden hoarders

Now target what the storage screen reveals:

**Messages:** In **Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Messages**, you'll see your heaviest conversations. Open one, tap the contact name at the top, and there's usually a **See All** view of photos, videos, and attachments you can delete in bulk. Or go into a specific conversation, tap and hold any attachment, and delete old ones.

**Downloads and offline content:** Check the **Files** app → **Browse → Downloads**, and clear out anything you don't need. Music, podcasts, and streaming apps that you downloaded for offline use are also easy to forget — delete the downloads you've already listened to.

**Offload unused apps:** In the iPhone Storage list, tap an app you rarely use and choose **Offload App**. It removes the app but keeps its documents and data, so reinstall is painless. Do this for the apps that take up the most room and are least used.

**Clear Safari data:** **Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data** frees cached pages. You'll lose saved browsing history but not your bookmarks.

## Step 5: Give System Data a nudge

There's no button to clear System Data directly, but these things reliably shrink it:

1. **Restart your iPhone.** A simple power cycle forces iOS to dump temporary files. Do this first — it's free.
2. **Update iOS.** Apple's updates clean up the system and often reset the caches. **Settings → General → Software Update.**
3. **Free enough space for the system to breathe.** Ironically, once you've freed a few gigabytes above, System Data often shrinks on its own as iOS runs its housekeeping.

## Wrapping up

The "storage is full even after deleting photos" mystery is usually just three overlooked bins: the 30-day photo trash, Messages attachments, and cached System Data. Empty Recently Deleted, turn on Optimize iPhone Storage, trim Messages, and restart — in that order.
