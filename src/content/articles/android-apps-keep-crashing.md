"Unfortunately, [app] has stopped." If that pop-up has been showing up more and more, you're not alone — it's one of the most common Android annoyances there is, and it's almost never a sign that your phone is dying.

Crashes are a software problem with software solutions. The fixes below are ordered like a ladder: start with the quick, safe ones and climb only as high as you need. Most people solve it on the first or second rung.

## Why apps crash in the first place

Apps crash for a handful of recurring reasons:

- **Outdated apps or an outdated Android version** — new apps expect newer software, and old software gets buggy.
- **A buggy update** — sometimes an update ships with a bug, and the app crashes until the developer fixes it.
- **Corrupted cache** — cached data goes bad and the app trips over it.
- **Very low storage** — the phone can't write the temporary files an app needs.
- **A conflict with another app** — occasionally one bad app crashes others around it.

That last point is why a restart often fixes things: it clears the bad state and lets everything start fresh.

## Fix 1: Restart the phone

The oldest fix in the book, and still the most effective first move. A restart ends stuck processes, clears corrupted temporary state, and gives every app a clean start.

1. Press and hold the power button.
2. Tap **Restart** (or turn the phone off, wait a few seconds, and turn it on).
3. Use the phone normally and see if the crashes stop.

If crashes are happening across multiple apps, do this before anything else.

## Fix 2: Update the app — and your Android version

If one specific app keeps crashing, it may already be fixed in a newer version.

1. Open the **Google Play Store**.
2. Tap your profile icon → **Manage apps & device**, and update the crashing app (or tap **Update all**).
3. Also check for a system update: **Settings → System → System update**.

Old software running newer apps is a recipe for crashes. Updating covers both sides of that equation.

## Fix 3: Clear the app's cache

Corrupted cache is a classic crash cause, and clearing it is fast and completely safe — it won't delete your accounts, messages, or settings.

1. Open **Settings → Apps**.
2. Tap the crashing app.
3. Tap **Storage & cache → Clear cache**.

If that doesn't help, the next rung is **Clear storage** (called "Clear data" on some phones). This resets the app to a fresh install — it logs you out and removes local content — so back up anything important first. Our [guide to clearing app cache on Android](/articles/how-to-clear-cache-android) explains the difference between the two in detail.

## Fix 4: Free up storage

When storage is nearly full, Android struggles to write the temporary files apps need to run, and crashes become common. It's a sneaky cause, because the phone looks fine until apps start dropping.

- Check your storage: **Settings → Storage**.
- If you're below roughly 15% free, free up space before anything else.
- The biggest wins are photos, videos, and unused apps — see [how to free up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) for the full rundown.

## Fix 5: Uninstall and reinstall the app

If a single app still crashes after an update and cache clear, a clean reinstall often resets whatever got corrupted.

1. In the Play Store, open the app's page and tap **Uninstall**, or press and hold the app icon → **Uninstall**.
2. Reinstall it from the Play Store.
3. Sign back in and test.

Remember: uninstalling wipes the app's local data, so game progress and offline content are gone unless backed up. For most social, banking, and productivity apps, a sign-in restores everything.

## Fix 6: Use safe mode to isolate a bad app

When crashes keep coming even after the fixes above, the culprit may be a specific downloaded app — possibly one you don't suspect. Safe mode is how you find out.

Safe mode boots the phone with **only the system's built-in apps**. Everything you downloaded is temporarily paused, and if it's a third-party app causing the trouble, the crashes stop while you're in safe mode.

**How to enter safe mode (varies by brand):**

- On most phones: press and hold the **power button**, then press and hold **Power off** on the screen until a "Reboot to safe mode" prompt appears. Tap it.
- On some Samsung phones: turn the phone off, then hold **Power** and **Volume Down** together during startup.

While in safe mode, watch for crashes. If they stop, the problem is one of your apps — uninstall recently installed or suspicious apps until it's fixed. To leave safe mode, simply restart the phone normally. The exact keys differ between brands, so if the above doesn't work for your model, search "safe mode" for your phone brand — it's a standard, widely documented feature.

## Fix 7: When one specific app is the only problem

If every fix above works everywhere except a single stubborn app, the problem is that app, not your phone:

- **Check the app's Play Store reviews** — if everyone's complaining about crashes since the last update, it's a known bug and a fix is coming.
- **Make sure it's up to date** — developers ship crash fixes in updates.
- **Clear its data** (Settings → Apps → the app → Storage → Clear storage) as the last app-level option — it resets the app fully.
- **If it's a banking or essential app**, try contacting the app's support; they can tell you if it's a known issue with your Android version.

## Fix 8: Factory reset — the last resort

If crashes persist across multiple apps after all of the above, a factory reset wipes the phone clean and starts over. It's drastic, but it's very effective against software rot that nothing else touches.

1. Back up everything — photos, messages, and anything you can't afford to lose.
2. Open **Settings → System → Reset options → Erase all data (factory reset)**.
3. Set the phone up fresh and reinstall apps gradually, watching for when crashes return.

A factory reset almost always resolves software-caused crashes. If the phone still crashes constantly after a clean reset, that points to hardware, and it's time for professional help. ## Wrapping up

Work the ladder: restart, update, clear cache, free storage, reinstall, safe mode — and a factory reset only if you must. In most cases a restart plus an update sweep solves it in minutes. Safe mode is your diagnostic tool for finding a misbehaving app, and reinstalling fixes most single-app problems.

Crashes are annoying, but they're almost always software, and software is fixable. The steps above cover nearly every cause, in the right order.
