A screen that goes black or a phone that won't turn on feels like a disaster. It usually isn't. In a large share of cases it's a software freeze or a deeply drained battery — both of which are fixable in minutes, at home, for free.

The trick is working through the fixes in the right order: safest first, most involved last. Here's the ladder, starting with the two things that solve most black-screen cases.

## Fix 1: Do a forced restart

If the phone froze or the software crashed, the screen can stay black even though the phone is technically on. A normal power press won't help — the phone is too stuck to respond. That's what the forced restart is for.

1. Press and hold the **power button** for **10 to 30 seconds**. Keep holding even if nothing happens at first.
2. On many phones, you can also press and hold **power + volume down** together.
3. Wait — the phone should vibrate or show its logo, then reboot normally.

This is called a soft reset or forced restart. It does not delete your photos, apps, or anything else — it just forces a stuck phone to reboot. It's the single most effective fix for a black screen caused by a software hang, so do it before anything else.

If the phone powers on but the screen stays black and won't respond to touch, do the forced restart again — some hangs take a couple of tries.

## Fix 2: Charge it and wait

A phone that drained to zero can sit on a black screen for a while before it even shows the charging icon. It looks completely dead — and it isn't.

1. Plug the phone into a **wall charger** with the cable and block that came with it, or another known-good set. Laptop USB ports and worn cables may not deliver enough power.
2. Leave it for **15 to 30 minutes**. Don't judge it at the two-minute mark.
3. After charging, try the forced restart from Fix 1 again.

If a charging icon appears, you're in business — it was just a drained battery. If nothing appears after half an hour on a good charger, the battery may be deeply drained or faulty, or the problem is elsewhere. A phone that won't hold a charge is worth learning more about — our [guide to making your Android battery last longer](/articles/extend-android-battery-life) covers battery health, and a dead battery is a common cause of "won't turn on."

## Fix 3: Figure out if the phone is actually on

The most useful question to answer is whether your phone is dead or just showing you nothing. If the screen is black but the phone is running, the fix is different — and the news is often better.

To test if the phone is on:

- **Call it.** From another phone, call your number and listen. If it rings or goes to voicemail after a few rings, the phone is on and the display is the problem.
- **Listen and feel.** A phone on but with a dead screen still vibrates for notifications, makes sound, and gets warm when it charges.
- **Plug it into a computer.** Connect it to a PC or Mac with a USB cable. If it appears as a device or drive in the file manager, the phone is definitely on — and the display hardware is the issue.

A phone that's "on but black" points to either a software freeze (fixable with the forced restart) or a display problem — a cracked screen, a loose display cable, or failed hardware. If the restart didn't bring the display back, the display itself is the suspect, and that's a repair-shop job (more below).

## Fix 4: Try safe mode (when the screen works but the system is stuck)

If the screen comes back but the phone is behaving badly — stuck on the logo, black after the boot animation, apps crashing in a loop — safe mode is your next tool. It boots the phone with only the system's built-in apps, so you can tell whether a downloaded app is causing the problem.

**How to enter safe mode (varies by brand):**

- On most phones: press and hold the **power button**, then press and hold **Power off** on the screen until a "Reboot to safe mode" prompt appears. Tap it.
- On some Samsung phones: turn the phone off, then hold **Power** and **Volume Down** together during startup.

If the phone boots and the screen works in safe mode, a downloaded app is the culprit — uninstall recently installed apps until it's fixed. To leave safe mode, restart normally. If it won't even reach safe mode, the problem is more likely the system or hardware.

## Fix 5: Software vs. hardware — and the recovery option

If the phone powers on, shows the logo, and then the screen goes black, that's a software or system problem. If the screen stays black with no logo, no vibration, and no sound even after a forced restart and charging, you're looking at hardware.

For software problems, there's one more level before calling a repair shop: Android's **recovery mode**. It's a hidden menu used for system-level actions like wiping the cache partition or doing a factory reset when the normal interface won't boot. The exact button combination varies by brand (commonly power + volume up or power + volume down during startup), so search "recovery mode" plus your phone's model if you want to try it.

A word of caution: only use recovery mode for the factory-reset option as a last resort, because a reset wipes your data — back everything up first if you can. Recovery mode is also easy to get wrong, and poking through it without knowing what you're doing can make things worse. If you're not comfortable, skip it and go to the professionals.

## Fix 6: Know when it's a hardware problem

Some causes of a black screen no software fix will touch:

- **A cracked or damaged screen.** If the phone was dropped or hit, the display (or its connection to the board) can fail even when the phone works fine underneath.
- **Water or liquid damage.** Even small amounts of liquid can kill the display.
- **A swollen battery.** If the back cover or screen is bulging, the battery has swollen — this is a safety concern. Stop charging it, power it off, and take it to a professional immediately. Do not press on it or try to open the phone.
- **Battery failure.** If the phone won't charge at all and shows no sign of life on a known-good charger, the battery may be dead.

## The caution section: don't open the phone

It's tempting to try to fix a screen yourself. Please don't. Modern Android phones are sealed and glued together, and the old internet "hacks" — baking a phone, freezing it, shaking it, or prying the screen open — are myths that usually cause more damage. Prying a phone open without the right tools cracks the glass, tears the display cable, and voids whatever warranty you have.

If it's a hardware problem, the realistic options are a professional repair shop or the manufacturer's service. A screen replacement is a normal, common repair, and reputable shops handle it safely. And if the phone was already slow, old, or failing before the screen died, it may be simpler to replace the phone itself — [our guide to fixing a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) can help you judge whether it's worth saving. More phone help is in the [Android category](/articles/category/android).

## Wrapping up

Work the ladder in order: forced restart first (most black screens are software freezes), then charge-and-wait for a drained battery, then figure out whether the phone is actually on. Software issues get safe mode and, as a last resort, a recovery-mode reset. Hardware issues — cracked displays, swollen batteries, water damage — get a professional.

Nine times out of ten, the forced restart or a good long charge brings the screen back. And when it doesn't, you now know exactly what to tell the repair shop.
