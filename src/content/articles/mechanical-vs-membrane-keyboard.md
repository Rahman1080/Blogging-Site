Here's the honest summary: mechanical keyboards feel more satisfying to type on, sound better (to most people), and last for years, but they cost more and make more noise. Membrane keyboards are cheaper, quieter, and perfectly fine for most people.

Which one you should buy depends on how much you type, whether the noise would drive your household (or office) up the wall, and what your budget allows. Here's the breakdown.

## How each keyboard works

### Membrane keyboards

A membrane keyboard is the classic office and laptop keyboard. Under each key is a small rubber dome on a sheet (membrane) of circuits. When you press a key, the dome collapses and completes a circuit. They're cheap to make, thin, quiet, and found on almost every laptop and budget desktop bundle.

### Mechanical keyboards

A mechanical keyboard has a real mechanical switch under each key — a spring-loaded mechanism that clicks as it actuates. Each key is its own independent unit, which is why they last longer and feel more consistent. They're thicker, heavier, more expensive, and louder.

## Feel and sound

This is the difference everyone actually cares about.

- **Feel.** A mechanical key gives you a crisp, physical "bump" or click as you press, with a spring that pushes back. Many people find it more satisfying and easier to type accurately on for long stretches. A membrane key feels mushier — the press bottoms out with less feedback.
- **Sound.** Membrane keyboards are quiet. Mechanical keyboards make a sound — everything from a soft thock to a loud click, depending on the switch. In a quiet office, a loud mechanical keyboard will be noticed. There are "silent" switch options that keep most of the feel with far less noise.

Neither is objectively better — it's preference. The way to know which you prefer is to try one.

## Durability and lifespan

Mechanical switches are rated for many more key presses than membrane keyboards, and because each switch is independent, a single worn-out key can often be replaced instead of scrapping the whole board. A quality mechanical keyboard is the kind of thing you can use for a decade. A membrane keyboard will eventually get mushy and is usually disposable when it wears out.

## Price

Mechanical keyboards cost more — often several times more than an equivalent membrane keyboard at the budget end, and much more for premium boards with fancy switches and metal cases. Membrane keyboards are cheap, which is exactly why they ship with most computers. If you're deciding between a basic membrane keyboard and a mid-range mechanical one, the gap is real but not enormous; the "is it worth it" question comes down to how much you type.

## Switch types, in plain terms

Mechanical switches come in three broad personalities, and this is what "switch types" actually means:

- **Linear** — smooth and consistent from top to bottom, no bump, quietest of the three. Popular with gamers, who want a clean, fast keystroke.
- **Tactile** — a gentle bump you can feel at the point where the key registers. A nice middle ground for typing, giving feedback without the click.
- **Clicky** — the bump plus an audible click, the classic "typewriter" sound. Great for people who love the noise; a dealbreaker for everyone else in the room.

If you're buying your first mechanical keyboard, tactile or linear are the safest starting points. Clicky is best tried before you commit.

## Who should buy mechanical

- You type a lot — long documents, code, email — and want more comfort and feedback.
- You enjoy the feel and sound and they won't annoy people around you.
- You want a keyboard that lasts for years and can be maintained, or you want a specific, satisfying typing experience.

## Who should stick with membrane

- You barely type and just need something that works.
- You work in a shared or quiet space and don't want noise.
- Your budget is tight and the difference wouldn't change your day.

There's also the laptop middle ground: you can't swap a laptop's built-in keyboard, but a budget membrane external keyboard is fine for occasional use.

## How to test before you buy

1. **Try one in a store.** A few minutes of typing tells you more than any review. Feel the difference in sound and feedback yourself.
2. **If you can't test, buy from somewhere with easy returns**, so you can live with it for a few days and send it back if the feel or noise isn't for you.
3. **Check the switch type** you're buying — linear, tactile, or clicky — and match it to the descriptions above.
4. **Read a review or two about the keyboard's sound**, since descriptions of "quiet" and "thocky" vary.

## Wrapping up

Mechanical keyboards aren't just a luxury toy for typing — they're a genuinely better feel that earns their higher price if you type often and can tolerate the sound. Membrane keyboards remain the sensible, quiet, budget choice for everyone else. Match the keyboard to how much you type and the noise you're allowed to make, and you'll be happy either way.
