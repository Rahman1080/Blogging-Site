A black screen is scary, but it usually isn't a broken iPhone. Most "won't turn on" cases come down to a drained battery, a charging quirk, or a frozen system — all fixable at home in a few minutes.

Work through this ladder in order, from the least invasive step to the most. Stop as soon as one of them works.

## 1. Rule out the charger and cable

Before blaming the phone, blame the cord. Cheap, frayed, or worn-out cables are the number one reason an iPhone doesn't charge, and they fail without warning.

1. Try a **different charger and a different cable** — ideally a known-good Apple or certified one.
2. Plug in and **watch for the charging icon**. No icon at all usually means no power is reaching the phone.
3. Clean the **charging port gently**. Lint and pocket fuzz build up at the bottom of the port and block the connector. Use a soft, dry toothpick or a plastic pick — never anything metal, and never blow into it.
4. Try **wireless charging** if you have a compatible pad. It bypasses the port entirely, which tells you whether the port or the cable was the problem.

If you see a charging icon but the screen stays black, move on to the next step.

## 2. Give a completely dead battery time

A battery that's been drained to zero can take a few minutes to show any sign of life. This is normal, not a fault.

Leave the phone plugged in for at least half an hour. After that, hold the side button and see if anything happens. If you still get nothing, try the force restart below — it works even when the phone looks completely dead.

## 3. Force restart the iPhone

A force restart is the iPhone equivalent of holding the power button on a stuck computer. It doesn't erase anything and it's safe to try as often as you like.

**On iPhone 8 and later (including the SE 2nd gen and newer):**

1. Press and quickly release the **Volume Up** button.
2. Press and quickly release the **Volume Down** button.
3. Press and **hold the side button** until the Apple logo appears. Keep holding — it can take longer than you expect, sometimes close to a minute.

**On iPhone 7 and iPhone 7 Plus:** hold the **Home** button and the **side button** together until you see the Apple logo.

**On iPhone 6s and earlier:** hold the **Home** button and the **top (or side) button** together until the Apple logo appears.

If the phone restarts, great. If it restarts but keeps looping on the Apple logo, jump to recovery mode below.

## 4. Charge it from a wall socket

If the phone only charges sometimes, the culprit is often a laptop USB port or a power strip that doesn't push enough power. Plug directly into a **wall socket** with the original-style power adapter, and skip any "fast charge" cables that aren't certified.

While you're at it, check that the cable is seated fully — a cable that's halfway in will connect and disconnect repeatedly, which the phone sometimes interprets as "don't charge."

## 5. Try recovery mode (the computer route)

If force restarting fails and the phone is stuck black or looping on the Apple logo, recovery mode is the standard software fix. It lets your computer talk to the phone directly.

**What you'll need:** a Mac, or a Windows PC with the Apple Devices app (or iTunes), plus a USB cable.

1. Connect the iPhone to the computer with the cable.
2. On a **Mac**, open **Finder** and select the iPhone under Locations. On a **Windows PC**, open **Apple Devices** or **iTunes**.
3. Put the phone into recovery mode:
   - **iPhone 8 and later:** press and quickly release Volume Up, then Volume Down, then press and hold the side button. Keep holding while the phone is connected — you should eventually see a computer-and-cable icon on screen.
   - **iPhone 7:** hold Home and the side button.
   - **Older models:** hold Home and the top button.
4. When your computer asks, choose **Update** rather than **Restore**. Update tries to reinstall iOS while keeping your data. If Update fails or the phone still won't boot, Restore erases and reinstalls everything — a last resort.
5. Wait. The process can take a while, and the phone will reboot on its own when it's done.

**Important honesty:** both Update and Restore can fail if the underlying hardware is damaged. And if you choose Restore, your data goes away — which is why the [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) exists. If your phone won't boot and you weren't backed up, a working data-recovery service is the only remaining option, and it's not cheap.

## When to stop and see Apple

Some situations aren't fixable at home, and trying more is just wasted effort. Contact Apple Support (or an Apple Store) if any of these apply:

- The phone was **dropped, bent, or got wet** right before it stopped working.
- You see a **temperature warning** or the phone is hot to the touch while plugged in.
- It **still won't respond** after recovery mode and a proper restore.
- It **won't charge on any cable or charger**, and the port is clean.

A phone that needs service can often be diagnosed quickly at an Apple Store, and you can book a Genius Bar appointment online.

## Wrapping up

Nine times out of ten, an iPhone that "won't turn on" is just an iPhone that's out of battery or frozen — fixable with a known-good cable and a force restart. Work the ladder: charger, time, force restart, wall socket, recovery mode. Only then start worrying about hardware.
