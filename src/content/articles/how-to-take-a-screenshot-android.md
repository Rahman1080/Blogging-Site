Screenshots are one of those things everyone needs occasionally and no one wants to fumble with. The good news: there's one shortcut that works on nearly every Android phone ever made, plus brand-specific gestures that are even faster once you know them.

Below is everything — the universal button combo, the no-button tricks, scrolling screenshots for long pages, and where your captures actually go.

## The universal method: power + volume down

This is the one that works on essentially every Android phone, from budget models to flagships, and it's the first thing to try no matter what brand you have.

1. Open whatever you want to capture.
2. Press and hold the **power button** and the **volume down button** at the same time.
3. Hold them for about a second — you'll see a quick flash or shutter animation, and a small preview thumbnail appears.
4. That's it. The screenshot is saved.

A few notes from real-world use: press both buttons at roughly the same moment, and don't press too early or the volume alone changes instead. On a few older Samsung models the combo used to be Home + power, but modern Samsung phones use power + volume down like everyone else.

## Screenshot without buttons

The button combo is reliable, but if your power button is broken, awkward, or you just want a shortcut, Android has several no-button options. Which ones you get depends on your brand and model — search the term in your phone's Settings if you don't see it below.

**Google Assistant** — works on nearly any Android phone:

1. Say **"Hey Google, take a screenshot"** (or open the Assistant).
2. The Assistant captures the current screen and lets you share or save it.

**Palm swipe (Samsung)** — the classic Galaxy trick:

1. Open **Settings → Advanced features → Motions and gestures**.
2. Turn on **Palm swipe to capture**.
3. With the phone in one hand, sweep the side of your other hand across the screen edge-to-edge.

**Three-finger swipe (many brands)** — available on many Xiaomi, OnePlus, realme, and other phones:

1. Open **Settings → Gestures** (or "Shortcuts and gestures").
2. Turn on **Three-finger screenshot** or the equivalent.
3. Swipe down the screen with three fingers at once.

**Screenshot quick tile** — some phones include a screenshot button in the notification shade or power menu. Pull down twice, look for a screenshot icon, and tap it.

## Scrolling screenshots (for long pages)

Need to capture a whole conversation, article, or web page — not just the visible screen? Most modern Android phones can stitch a long screenshot together.

1. Take a normal screenshot with any method above.
2. In the preview toolbar that appears briefly, look for a **Scroll**, **Capture more**, or **Extended** button (the icon is usually two arrows pointing down).
3. Tap it and the phone captures the page automatically, extending the image until you stop it or it reaches the end.
4. Tap **Save** (or the checkmark) when it looks complete.

The honest caveat: scrolling screenshots work in browsers, messaging apps, and most common apps, but not everywhere. Some apps block them by design (often for privacy reasons), and if the toolbar doesn't offer a scroll option, that page simply can't be captured that way.

## Where screenshots are saved

Every screenshot gets saved automatically — no "save as" step required. You'll find them in one or more of these places:

- **Google Photos** — open the app → **Library → Screenshots**.
- **Gallery apps** (Samsung Gallery, Xiaomi Gallery, etc.) — there's usually a **Screenshots** album.
- **File managers** — under **Pictures → Screenshots**, or DCIM → Screenshots on some phones.

You can edit right after capturing: the preview thumbnail that appears after a screenshot usually has buttons to crop, draw, or annotate before you save or share. If you miss it, both Google Photos and most Gallery apps let you edit the saved image later.

## Capturing only part of the screen

You don't always need the whole screen. Most phones let you crop right in the preview toolbar, but some brands go further with a partial-capture mode:

- **Samsung Smart Select** — pull out the edge panel, tap **Smart Select**, then draw a box or lasso around the area you want. It captures just that region, ready to share.
- **Pixel and others** — after taking a screenshot, the toolbar usually offers a **Crop** button; the captured image is trimmed before it's saved.
- **Screenshot in the toolbar** — the quick tiles on some phones open a mode where you draw the region before capturing.

Cropping to just what you need saves space in your gallery and makes shared screenshots clearer — no wasted black bars or stray notifications.

## When screenshots come back blank

A screenshot that saves as a black or empty image isn't a broken phone. Banking apps, streaming services, and some secure apps deliberately block screenshots to protect sensitive content — the capture either fails or saves a blank frame. You'll also see this on some login screens and in apps with "secure" flags turned on. It's the app's choice, not a defect, and there's no legitimate way around it.

Two other causes worth knowing:

- **Storage is full.** A screenshot needs space to save; if the phone has almost none, the capture fails silently. Free up a little space and try again.
- **A stuck app or overlay.** If the shortcut suddenly stops working across the board, restart the phone — a restart clears the glitch that stops the button combo or gesture from responding.

## Managing the screenshot pile

Screenshots accumulate fast — a few a day adds up to a cluttered gallery and wasted space before you notice. Since they live in their own folder, they're easy to manage:

- Delete screenshots you don't need from the Screenshots album in Google Photos or your Gallery app.
- Screenshots taken of long pages are usually large files — delete the ones you've already shared or used.
- If your phone is low on space, screenshots are a safe first thing to clear. Our [guide to freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) covers it alongside the bigger space hogs.

## Wrapping up

Remember the universal combo — power + volume down — and you can screenshot on any Android phone you ever pick up. Then level up with palm swipe on Samsung, three-finger swipe on the brands that offer it, or the Assistant on anything. Scrolling screenshots handle the long pages, and everything lands in your Screenshots album automatically.

Screenshots are one of those skills that take ten seconds to learn and save you forever after. Now you've got it.
