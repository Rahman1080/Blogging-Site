Phone storage fills up faster than anyone expects — especially with photos, videos, and chat apps. In most cases you can reclaim gigabytes in a few minutes without deleting anything important.

These methods work on both Android and iPhone. Start with the biggest wins.

## 1. See what's actually using space

You can't fix what you can't measure.

- **Android:** **Settings → Storage**. You'll see how much Photos, Videos, Apps, and Other use.
- **iPhone:** **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**. You'll get a bar chart plus per-app breakdowns.

Note the two biggest categories — that's where your space went.

## 2. Clear the Recently Deleted folder (biggest hidden win)

Photos you "delete" don't leave your phone for 30 days — they sit in a trash folder.

- **Android (Google Photos):** open the app → Library → Trash → Empty trash.
- **iPhone:** Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted → Delete All.

If you cleared out hundreds of photos recently, emptying the trash can instantly free gigabytes.

## 3. Offload photos and videos to the cloud

The biggest space hogs are usually videos. Moving them off-device reclaims space without losing anything.

- **Google Photos** (Android): enable **Backup & sync**, then use **Free up space** in the app — it removes on-device copies of backed-up photos and videos.
- **iCloud Photos** (iPhone): **Settings → Photos → Optimize iPhone Storage** keeps smaller versions on the phone.
- Third-party options: one of the major cloud drives with automatic camera upload.

> Tip: Review your video clips — they're often the single largest files on the phone. Back them up, then delete the originals.

## 4. Clean up chat apps

WhatsApp, Telegram, and Signal store every photo, video, and voice note you've ever received.

- **WhatsApp (Android):** **Settings → Storage and data → Manage storage**, then delete the biggest items and forwards.
- **WhatsApp (iPhone):** **Settings → Storage** has the same per-chat cleanup.
- **Telegram:** **Settings → Storage → Auto-delete media** for messages older than a set time.

## 5. Clear cached data

Cache helps apps load faster, but it grows endlessly.

- **Android:** **Settings → Apps** → pick large apps → **Storage & cache → Clear cache**.
- **iPhone:** iOS manages cache automatically, but you can offload unused apps: **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** → tap an app → **Offload App** (keeps documents and data, removes the app, and re-installs on demand).

## 6. Remove old downloads and unused apps

- **Android / iPhone:** Open the **Files** app (or Files by Google) and clear the Downloads folder.
- **iPhone:** In iPhone Storage, apps are sorted by size — delete the ones you haven't opened in months.
- **Android:** **Settings → Apps → All apps** and uninstall unused ones.

## 7. Check messages and offline maps

- **SMS/MMS:** photos in your text threads add up. Set auto-delete for old messages if your phone supports it.
- **Offline maps:** Google Maps downloads can be large. **Maps → Offline maps** and remove ones you don't use.
- **Music:** downloaded playlists are huge — stream instead, or keep only your favorites offline.

## 8. Use your phone's built-in cleanup tools

- **Android:** **Settings → Storage → Free up space / Smart Storage** — clears temporary files and offers cleanup recommendations.
- **iPhone:** iOS Storage suggestions offer exactly these cleanups.

## 9. Still full? The nuclear options

- **Android:** **Settings → System → Reset options → Erase all data** — wipes everything (back up first!).
- **iPhone:** **Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Erase All Content and Settings**.
- **Add storage:** some Android phones accept an SD card (check your model). iPhones don't.

## Wrapping up

Empty the trash, offload photos and videos, clean chat apps, and clear cache — in that order. You'll typically free up several gigabytes in under ten minutes, and your phone will feel faster too.

A phone that's constantly out of space is often a phone that's slow.
