# How to Clear the Cache on Android and iPhone Apps

Every app you use quietly stores temporary files: thumbnails, previews, half-downloaded images, and the bits of data that make it load faster next time. That's the cache, and it's mostly a good thing. But a bloated cache can cause weird glitches and eat storage that's better spent elsewhere.

Clearing it is simple on Android, and on iPhone it takes a slightly different path because iOS hides the direct button. Here's how to do both, plus the honest explanation of what clearing cache does and doesn't touch.

## What cache actually is (and what it isn't)

The cache is the app's short-term memory. When you scroll a social feed, the app saves the images it just showed you so it doesn't have to re-download them. When you open a web page, the browser keeps a copy of the site's images and scripts. All of that lives in the cache.

Here's the distinction that matters:

- **Cache** — temporary files that can be rebuilt. Safe to clear, and clearing it never touches your logins, documents, or settings.
- **Data** — the app's actual saved information: your account details, preferences, downloads, drafts. Clearing this wipes the app back to like-new and signs you out.

Most of the time, cache is the one you want. Clearing data is a last resort for a truly broken app.

## Clear the cache on Android

Android gives you a direct, per-app cache button. The path is nearly identical across phone brands.

1. Open **Settings → Apps** (sometimes called **Apps & notifications** or **Application manager**).
2. Scroll to the app you want, or tap **See all apps** if it's not listed.
3. Tap **Storage and cache** (some phones just say **Storage**).
4. Tap **Clear cache**.

That's the whole process. The app's cache empties, your logins and settings stay, and the app rebuilds its cache over the next few uses. If the app was misbehaving, this often fixes it. If you want the full reset, **Clear storage** (or **Clear data**) is the option beside it — use that only when you're willing to sign back in and set the app up again.

Some phones also offer a one-tap "Clear all caches" or a storage cleanup tool in Settings. Those are fine to use, but they treat every app the same. The per-app method is more precise, which matters if you want to keep a particular app's cache for offline use — for example, an offline maps app.

## Clear the cache on iPhone

Apple doesn't expose a "clear cache" button per app, and it's not an oversight — iOS manages app storage more aggressively on its own. But you still have two solid options when an app is bloated or glitchy.

**Option 1: Offload the app.** This is Apple's closest equivalent, and it's clever because it keeps your data.

1. Open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**.
2. Find the app in the list (you'll see how much space it's using).
3. Tap the app, then tap **Offload App**.
4. Confirm. The app icon stays on your home screen, the app itself is removed, and the cache goes with it — but your documents and data are kept.
5. Tap the icon to reinstall the app, and your data comes back.

**Option 2: Delete and reinstall.** For an app that's genuinely broken, this is the full reset.

1. Tap and hold the app icon, tap **Remove App**, then **Delete App**.
2. Reinstall it from the App Store.

The difference from offloading: delete-and-reinstall also wipes the app's data, so you'll sign in again. It's the iPhone equivalent of Android's "Clear storage," so only use it when you're prepared to log back in.

**For Safari specifically,** iOS does offer a direct cache clear: **Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data**. That's the one built-in, per-app cache reset on iPhone, and it's useful for fixing a misbehaving browser without touching the rest of the system.

## When clearing cache actually helps

Clearing cache is a fix, not a hobby. It helps in three situations:

- **An app misbehaves** — crashes on launch, shows blank screens, or loads content that's clearly outdated. A stale cache is often the culprit.
- **An app's storage usage is absurd** — some apps' caches balloon into gigabytes, especially browsers, video apps, and social feeds.
- **You're freeing space fast** — clearing a few bloated caches can recover storage in minutes without deleting anything you care about.

It does *not* help with overall phone speed the way people often expect. Caches make apps faster; clearing them doesn't speed up your phone, and apps rebuild them anyway. If your phone genuinely feels slow, the causes and fixes are different — and our guide to [fixing a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) walks through the ones that work.

## What to do when storage is the real problem

Cache clearing recovers a little space here and there. When storage is genuinely full, the bigger wins come from removing what you actually don't need: old photos, unused apps, downloaded videos. Our guide to [freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) covers the full clean-up, and the dedicated deep-dive on [clearing cache on Android](/articles/how-to-clear-cache-android) has every variation for that platform.

## Wrapping up

The cache is your apps' temporary memory — safe to clear, useful to clear when something misbehaves, and never a threat to your logins or documents. Android gives you the direct button; on iPhone, offload or reinstall instead. Clear selectively, keep your data, and save the heavy clean-up for when it actually matters.
