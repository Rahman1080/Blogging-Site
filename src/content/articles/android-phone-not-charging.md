A phone that won't charge is one of the most stressful tech problems there is — but it's also one of the most fixable. Before you panic, trade it in, or pay for a repair, work through the fixes below in order. They go from the most common cause to the most involved, and most people solve the problem within the first few steps.

The odds are in your favor: charging problems are usually caused by something simple, like a worn cable, a dirty port, or a charger that isn't delivering power the way it used to.

## What you'll need

- The phone's original charger and cable, or a known-good spare
- A second cable or charger if you have one
- A soft-bristled toothbrush or a clean wooden toothpick
- A wall outlet you know works
- A few minutes of patience

## 1. Check the basics: cable, charger, and outlet

This single step solves more charging problems than anything else. A cable that looks perfectly fine on the outside can be broken inside — they get bent, twisted, and stepped on, and the damage is invisible.

1. Plug your phone into a **wall outlet** you know works. Laptop USB ports and car chargers often can't deliver enough power.
2. Try a **different cable**. If the phone charges with the new one, the old cable was the problem.
3. Try a **different charger block** if you have one. Some blocks fail quietly — they still look fine but deliver almost no power.
4. Leave it plugged in for at least a few minutes before you judge. A completely drained phone can sit there for a while before it shows any charging icon.

If the phone charges but does so slowly, the same checklist applies: use the charger that came with the phone, skip the computer's USB port, and stop using the phone while it's plugged in. Fast charging needs a compatible charger and cable, and any weak link in that chain slows everything down.

## 2. Clean the charging port

Lint and pocket dust are the second most common cause, and they're sneaky. Over months, tiny fibers pack into the bottom of the port until the plug no longer seats fully. The cable looks connected — it clicks in — but the contacts barely touch, so the phone charges slowly, intermittently, or not at all.

1. **Power the phone off.**
2. Look into the port with a light. You'll often see a visible wad of lint.
3. Gently work it out with a **soft-bristled toothbrush** or a **clean wooden toothpick**. Angle it along the edge of the connector, not straight down into the center.
4. Gently blow out any remaining dust, then plug the cable back in and make sure it seats fully.

A few cautions: never use a metal pin, paperclip, or screwdriver — the pins inside the port are fragile, and a short can damage the phone. Never blow hard into the port (moisture from your breath is worse than dust). If you're not confident, a phone repair shop will clean a port safely for a small fee.

## 3. Restart the phone

Software can throw a fit that stops charging even when the hardware is perfect. A restart clears that kind of glitch in seconds.

1. Hold the power button.
2. Choose **Restart** (or turn it off, wait a moment, and turn it back on).
3. Plug the phone back in once it's running.

A phone that only charges when it's off is a clue that something on the running system is interfering — often a faulty app or a pending update. Restart first, then update your apps and Android version, and check your battery usage screen for an app that's misbehaving. If it still refuses to charge while on, the battery itself is the more likely suspect (see step 6).

## 4. Try wireless charging, if your phone supports it

Many modern Android phones charge wirelessly as well as by cable. If yours does, this is a useful test: place the phone on a wireless charging pad and see what happens.

- If it charges wirelessly but not by cable, the port or cable is the problem — and you've just confirmed it.
- If it charges by cable but not wirelessly, the coil or the pad is at fault, which is usually a separate issue.

This test costs nothing and tells you which half of the system to focus on. You can also keep using wireless charging while you sort out the port.

## 5. Check battery protection settings

If your phone charges fine but stops at a certain percentage, don't assume a fault. Many phones ship with a battery protection feature that deliberately stops charging at around 80% or 85% to slow battery aging.

- Look in **Settings → Battery → Battery protection** (the name varies by brand) for a "protect battery" or "limit to 85%" toggle.
- If it's on and you want a full charge, turn it off.
- If the phone stops at an odd percentage and the setting is off, the battery is likely worn — see the next step.

Charging that stalls below a few percent, or that resets the percentage down after you unplug, also points to a tired battery rather than a cable issue.

## 6. Consider the battery itself

If you've ruled out the cable, charger, port, and software, the battery may simply be at the end of its life. Batteries wear out — it's normal, and it usually happens after a few years of daily use.

Signs of a worn battery include a phone that dies well above 1%, shuts off randomly, or won't hold a charge for a reasonable amount of time. A battery replacement from a reputable shop is typically far cheaper than a new phone and can restore a full day of use. Our [guide to making your Android battery last longer](/articles/extend-android-battery-life) covers the daily habits that keep a healthy battery healthy for as long as possible.

## 7. Factory reset as a last resort

Rarely, corrupted system settings block charging. A factory reset wipes everything and restores the phone to a clean state, so back up your photos, messages, and anything else you care about first.

1. Back up your data to your Google account and a cloud photo service.
2. Open **Settings → System → Reset options → Erase all data (factory reset)**.
3. Set the phone up fresh and test charging again before reinstalling your apps one by one.

If a factory reset doesn't fix it, the problem is hardware, and software fixes won't help. Before you reach that point, our [guide to fixing a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) is worth a read — a phone that charges poorly and runs slowly often has the same underlying cause.

## When to get professional help

Take the phone to a repair shop or the manufacturer when:

- It won't charge at all after trying all of the above.
- The port is physically damaged or wobbly.
- The phone has been dropped, bent, or exposed to water.
- The back cover or screen is bulging — that can indicate a swollen battery, which is a safety concern, not just a charging problem. Power the phone off and have it handled by a professional. Do not try to open it yourself.

## Wrapping up

Start cheap and easy: test another cable, another charger, and another outlet, then clean the port. That covers the vast majority of charging problems. If the hardware checks out, move through restart, wireless-charging test, and battery settings before you spend a cent. Only then consider the battery, a reset, or a repair — and know that a worn battery is a normal fix, not a sign your phone is finished.
