A laptop that won't turn on is alarming, but it's very often not the disaster it feels like. The most common causes — a charger that stopped charging, a battery too drained to notice, or a laptop stuck in a weird state — have simple fixes that take two minutes.

Work through this list in order. It's deliberately structured: the easiest, most common checks come first, and you'll only reach the hardware-repair conclusion after ruling everything else out.

## Step 1: Confirm the power situation

Most "dead" laptops are actually just uncharged or unpowered. Before anything else:

1. **Plug the charger into the wall** (not a power strip that might be switched off) and into the laptop.
2. **Look for a charging light** near the charging port. On many laptops, a small light comes on or changes color when charging.
3. **If there's no light**, try a different outlet, then a different cable if you have a spare.

A laptop whose battery completely drained can take a few minutes on the charger before the power button responds at all. If you've been pressing it during that window, stop, let it charge for **five to ten minutes**, and try once more.

## Step 2: Check the obvious hardware

- **Is the charger plugged into the laptop fully?** The barrel or USB-C plug can sit half-out. Give it a firm push.
- **Is the charging port damaged?** Look inside for bent pins or debris. A loose port that only charges when the cable is held at an angle means a repair is coming eventually.
- **Is the battery seated?** If your laptop has a removable battery (common on older budget models), take it out, check the contacts, and reinsert it firmly.

## Step 3: Do a hard reset

When a laptop gets stuck, a hard reset clears the leftover static charge and gives the hardware a clean slate. This genuinely fixes many "won't turn on" cases:

1. **Unplug** the charger.
2. **Remove the battery** if it's removable.
3. **Hold the power button down for 15–20 seconds** (you may see a flicker or hear a click — that's expected).
4. **Put the battery back**, plug in the charger, and press the power button normally.

If the battery isn't removable, just unplug the charger and hold the power button for 15–20 seconds, then plug back in and try.

## Step 4: Listen and look

Press the power button and pay attention to what the laptop does. That tells you where the problem lives:

- **Nothing at all — no light, no sound, no fan.** Power isn't reaching the machine. Re-check the charger, outlet, and port, then consider that the charger itself may have died.
- **Fan spins, lights come on, but no display.** The laptop is powered — it's just not showing anything. This is a display problem, not a power problem. See Step 5.
- **It starts, then shuts off again.** Could be overheating, a failed battery, or a deeper hardware issue — the kind a repair shop should look at.

## Step 5: Test with an external monitor

If the fan runs and you hear the laptop doing *something*, but the screen stays black, connect it to a TV or monitor (HDMI or the adapter for your port — our [guide to connecting a laptop to a TV](/articles/how-to-connect-laptop-to-tv) covers the cable part). If the external screen shows your desktop, the laptop itself is fine and the built-in screen needs repair. If nothing appears externally either, it's a deeper issue.

While you're at it: check that **brightness isn't set to zero**. A laptop can power on "silently" with a perfectly black screen if the brightness key got pressed to minimum. Tap the brightness-up key a few times and see.

## Step 6: A reset of the software layer

If the laptop powers on but won't boot properly, that's a separate problem from "won't turn on." A few options worth trying, from least to most involved:

- **Windows:** use the repair tools built into the recovery environment (often accessible by powering on and pressing a recovery key, or from a Windows install USB).
- **Mac:** try starting in safe mode or using macOS Recovery.

Software won't-boot problems are common enough that they deserve their own article, but for a machine that's completely dark, the steps above are the right ones.

## When it's honestly time for a repair

You've now ruled out the charger, the outlet, the battery, the stuck state, and the display. If the laptop still won't power on at all — no lights, no fan, no sound — the fault is internal: likely the power board, the motherboard, or the battery itself. That's where DIY stops for most people.

Before you pay for a repair, weigh it honestly:

- **How old is the laptop?** A repair on an old machine can cost more than the machine is worth. If it's aging, a replacement may be the better call — and our [laptop buying guide](/articles/how-to-choose-a-laptop-buying-guide) helps you avoid overpaying for the next one.
- **Is it the battery?** A genuinely dead battery is often the cheapest and easiest fix, and it's also the most common reason a laptop that "won't turn on" works fine when plugged in.

Worth knowing for next time: batteries that are never allowed to charge properly fail sooner. The [laptop battery guide](/articles/laptop-battery-draining-fast) explains healthy charging habits and how to check battery health before it strands you.

## Wrapping up

Charge it properly and wait, check the cable and port, do a hard reset, and test the display with an external monitor. Most laptops come back with one of those. If none of them work, that's the honest point where a repair shop — or a replacement — is the right answer.
