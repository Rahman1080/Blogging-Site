# How to Reduce Mobile Data Usage on Android and iPhone

Your mobile data is disappearing for a few familiar reasons: video apps streaming in the background, social feeds refreshing while you're not looking, and automatic app updates you forgot you turned on. The good news is that most of that usage is optional. With about ten minutes of settings changes, you can cut your mobile data usage noticeably without giving up anything you actually use.

The strategy has three parts, and the order matters: see what's eating your data, restrict the background stuff, then knock down the heavy hitters like video. Here's how to do it on both Android and iPhone.

## First, find the data usage screen

You can't reduce what you can't measure. Both platforms keep a running tally of how much mobile data each app has used, and it's the fastest way to spot the culprit.

**On Android:** Open **Settings → Network & internet → Data usage** (the exact wording varies slightly by phone brand). You'll see a chart of recent usage and, below it, a list of apps sorted by how much data they've used. Tap any app for a breakdown of foreground versus background usage.

**On iPhone:** Open **Settings → Cellular**. Near the top you'll see total data used in the current period, and below that, every app with its own number. You can also reset the counter when your billing cycle starts so the list stays useful.

Look for the surprises first — apps you barely open but that are quietly at the top of the list are almost always background data.

## Stop apps from using background data

This is the single highest-impact change, because it stops the usage you never see. An app running in the background can refresh feeds, sync files, and download updates without you touching it.

**On Android:** In the data usage list, tap the offending app and turn off **Background data**. The app still works normally when you open it — it just stops doing things on its own. If a phone maker's data screen is tucked away, search "background data" in Settings and you'll land on the same controls.

**On iPhone:** Open **Settings → Cellular**, scroll to the app, and toggle it off. This removes the app's mobile data access entirely, so it can't use background data or much of anything else without Wi-Fi. You can toggle individual apps back on any time — useful for the maps app before a road trip.

If an app genuinely needs to be live in the background (messaging, a music app for offline playlists), leave it alone. The goal is to cut the apps that don't need constant access.

## Turn on data saver mode

Both platforms have a one-switch "data saver" that tells apps to reduce their background activity and slim down what they download. It's a blunt but effective tool.

- **Android:** **Settings → Network & internet → Data saver**, then turn it on. You can allow specific apps through the restriction.
- **iPhone:** The same control lives under **Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Options → Low Data Mode**, and it's worth turning on per-app too — e.g., **Settings → Cellular → [app] → Low Data Mode**.

Data saver doesn't block anything completely. It just makes apps leaner about what they fetch until you open them.

## Lower video and streaming quality

Streaming video is the biggest single data consumer on any phone, by a wide margin. The fix isn't to stop watching — it's to lower the resolution, because most phones can't display the difference anyway.

- **YouTube and most video apps:** look for a **Data saver** or **Video quality** option in the app's settings and pick a lower resolution while on mobile data.
- **Music streaming:** apps like Spotify and Apple Music have a quality setting that's usually "stream over Wi-Fi only" for high-quality audio, and a lower bitrate option for cellular.
- **Video calls:** many calling apps let you lower video quality, which makes a real difference on longer calls.

## Keep updates on Wi-Fi only

Automatic app updates are convenient, but a batch of large updates over mobile data can eat a month's allowance in minutes. Both stores let you switch updates to Wi-Fi only.

- **Android:** Open **Google Play Store → Profile icon → Settings → Network preferences → Auto-update apps** and choose **Over Wi-Fi only**.
- **iPhone:** **Settings → App Store** and turn off **Automatic Updates**, or leave them on but rely on the separate **App Downloads** option, which can be set to **Wi-Fi only**.

The same logic applies to backups. iCloud backups and photo backups can also be set to Wi-Fi only, which is worth doing if you back up over cellular.

## Use Wi-Fi wherever you can

This one sounds obvious, but it's the cheapest data saver of all. Your phone treats saved Wi-Fi networks as home, so connect to home, work, and trusted public networks (with a bit of caution) and your phone will happily use them. If you're on a limited plan, make a habit of joining Wi-Fi before streaming or downloading anything large.

## Wrapping up

Reducing mobile data comes down to three moves: find out which apps are using the most, stop the background usage you never see, and drop streaming quality while you're on cellular. Do those and you'll likely stretch your plan further than any paid "unlimited" add-on would have.
