# Apps Not Updating on Android or iPhone: How to Fix

Every app update failing at once, or one app stuck on "Waiting," is one of the most common phone frustrations — and also one of the most fixable. The causes are almost always the same few things: a connection problem, a full storage drive, or the store app itself getting confused.

The fixes below run from the quickest to the most involved. Work through them in order and you'll rarely need to go past step three.

## The quick checks (do these first)

Before touching any settings, rule out the boring causes.

1. **Check your connection.** Updates need a working internet connection. Toggle Wi-Fi off and on, or switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data and try again. If one network is flaky, the other often isn't.
2. **Restart the phone.** The classic fix for a reason. A restart clears the stalled download state and forces the store to re-check. On most phones, holding the power button (or power + volume up) shows the restart option.
3. **Check the date and time.** Phones that are hours off from real time can fail to verify store connections. Open Settings → Date & time and confirm "Set automatically" is on.

If updates still fail, move to the platform-specific fixes.

## Fix apps not updating on Android

The Google Play Store keeps its own cache and handles downloads separately from your apps, so the fixes target it directly.

1. **Clear the Play Store's cache.** Open **Settings → Apps**, scroll to **Google Play Store**, tap **Storage and cache**, then **Clear cache**. Don't tap "Clear storage" yet — that also signs you out of the store. Try updates again.
2. **Force-stop the Play Store.** In the same screen, tap **Force stop**, then reopen the Play Store. This restarts the store's update process cleanly.
3. **Cancel and retry the stuck update.** In the Play Store, open **Manage apps and device → Updates**, and on the stuck app tap the download icon, then choose **Pause** and **Resume**. Some Android versions let you tap the "X" next to a download to cancel it, then tap **Update** again.
4. **Free up storage.** The Play Store will silently refuse updates when storage is nearly full. If an update error mentions insufficient space, it's time to clear out room — our guide to [freeing up phone storage](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) has the fast and safe way.
5. **Check your Google account sign-in.** Open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, and confirm you're signed in. If a payment method is expired or needs re-verification, the store may block updates to paid apps even if they were free.

## Fix apps not updating on iPhone

The App Store version of the same problems has its own ladder.

1. **Force the App Store to refresh.** Open the **App Store**, tap your profile picture at the top right, then pull the Updates list down to refresh it. Tap **Update All** if nothing was updating automatically.
2. **Cancel and restart the stuck update.** In the Updates list, tap the cloud icon or download indicator next to a stuck app to cancel it, wait a few seconds, then tap it again to retry. If the whole list is frozen, swipe the App Store away from the app switcher to close it, then reopen it.
3. **Restart the phone.** As with Android, a restart clears the stalled state reliably.
4. **Free up storage.** iPhone updates fail silently when storage is full. Check **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** to see what's taking space. Deleting a few large apps or old videos usually unblocks the queue.
5. **Check your Apple ID and network settings.** In **Settings → your name**, confirm your Apple ID is signed in. If you use a VPN or a restrictive network, the App Store can fail to reach Apple's servers — briefly disabling them is worth a test.

## Why apps get stuck on "Waiting" or "Paused"

That "Waiting" state is the store's download queue stalling, and it has a few common triggers:

- **A download was interrupted** — switching networks mid-download or losing signal. Cancel and retry.
- **The store is waiting on you** — a password prompt, a payment verification, or a "review this app" dialog you never saw. Open the store and look for any pop-up or banner asking for confirmation.
- **Too many downloads at once** — some phones queue updates in a way that ties up the line. Update one app at a time instead of all at once.
- **The app itself is damaged** — if a specific app always fails while others succeed, delete it and reinstall the latest version from the store. This is the reliable last resort.

## When automatic updates quietly turn themselves off

If updates work when you press the button but never happen on their own, auto-update is off or restricted.

- **Android:** Open the Play Store → profile picture → **Settings → Network preferences → Auto-update apps** and choose **Over any network** (or **Over Wi-Fi only** if you want to save data).
- **iPhone:** **Settings → App Store** and make sure **App Updates** is on under Automatic Downloads.

And one habit worth knowing: if your phone feels sluggish in general and updates are part of a bigger slowdown, that's a separate problem. Our guide to [fixing a slow Android phone](/articles/android-phone-running-slow) covers the performance causes and fixes that are unrelated to app updates.

## Wrapping up

Apps not updating is almost never a sign of a broken phone. Check the connection, restart the device, clear the store's cache (Android) or refresh it (iPhone), free up storage, and confirm your account is signed in. If one stubborn app keeps failing, delete and reinstall it. Nine times out of ten, one of those fixes unblocks the whole queue.
