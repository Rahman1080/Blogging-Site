A slow iPhone isn't usually a dying iPhone. Nine times out of ten it's a full hard drive, a misunderstood habit, or a battery that's quietly aged — all fixable in an evening, and all much cheaper than a new phone.

Let's be honest about one thing first: an old iPhone won't ever feel as fast as a new one. But it can feel dramatically better than it does right now. Here's what actually works, in order of impact.

## 1. Free up storage (the biggest lever)

A nearly-full iPhone is a slow iPhone. iOS needs empty space for temporary files, updates, and background housekeeping — when it has none, everything crawls. Keeping 10–15% of your storage free is the rough rule of thumb.

1. Go to **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** and look at what's eating space.
2. Empty the **Recently Deleted** photo album (Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted → Delete All). This alone can free gigabytes.
3. **Offload unused apps** from the iPhone Storage list — it removes the app but keeps its data, so reinstalling is painless.
4. Trim the big stuff: Messages attachments, offline music downloads, old videos.

Our full [phone storage guide](/articles/free-up-storage-phone) walks through every method step by step. This is the single most effective thing you can do for a sluggish phone.

## 2. Stop force-quitting apps

If you habitually swipe apps away in the app switcher to "clear RAM," here's the news: it's doing the opposite of what you want.

iOS keeps apps suspended in the background, not running, so they can snap back instantly. When you force-quit an app, you force it to cold-start next time — which takes longer, uses more power, and makes the phone feel laggier. The phone's own memory management handles the rest.

The only time to force-quit is when an app is frozen or misbehaving. Otherwise, leave your apps alone and your iPhone will feel faster for it.

## 3. Update iOS (and your apps)

Apple ships performance fixes and memory-management improvements in almost every iOS update. If your phone is running something old, you're missing out on free speed.

- **Settings → General → Software Update** and install anything pending.
- Update your apps from the **App Store** while you're at it — developers optimize for the newest iOS, and stale apps are a common cause of sluggishness.

One honest note: after a big iOS update, the phone can be slower for a day or two while it reindexes photos and reoptimizes storage. Give it time before you judge.

## 4. Turn off Background App Refresh

Every app that refreshes in the background is quietly doing work. Trim the list.

1. **Settings → General → Background App Refresh**.
2. Set it to **Off**, or switch it off for apps that don't need live updates. Your email and messaging apps matter; the shopping apps don't.

This won't make the UI feel snappier in your hands, but it frees the processor and battery for what you're actually doing.

## 5. Reduce the visual effects

iOS animations are pretty, but on older hardware they can feel sluggish. Turning some down makes everything feel faster.

- **Settings → Accessibility → Motion → Reduce Motion** — trims the parallax and zoom effects.
- **Settings → Accessibility → Motion → Reduce Transparency** — lightens the translucent blur effects.

None of this hurts the phone; it just trades a bit of polish for responsiveness.

## 6. Check the battery health — and don't ignore it

Here's the part most people never learn: **iOS deliberately slows the processor on iPhones with a degraded battery** to prevent unexpected shutdowns. A battery at low health genuinely makes your phone slower.

1. Open **Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging**.
2. Look at **Maximum Capacity**. Around 80% or below is Apple's threshold for service.

If you're there, the honest fix isn't a settings tweak — it's a battery replacement. Apple swaps them in-store or by mail, and it's usually the best performance upgrade an older iPhone can get, for far less than a new phone.

## 7. Restart the phone once a week

A weekly restart is cheap maintenance. It clears accumulated background processes and gives the system a clean slate. It won't fix a fundamentally slow phone, but it smooths out the drift that builds up after weeks of use.

## What to do if it's still slow

If you've freed storage, updated iOS, stopped force-quitting, checked the battery, and the phone is still laggy, then be honest about what's left:

- **Very old hardware** is the limit. A phone from many generations back can't keep pace with modern apps, and no tweak changes that.
- A **factory reset** (Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Erase All Content and Settings) can clear deep-seated software gunk — but only after you've backed up, which the [iPhone backup guide](/articles/backup-iphone-guide) covers.
- If the phone is still miserable, an upgrade is the real answer — at least now you'll know you tried everything first.

## Wrapping up

The speed fixes that actually work are the boring ones: free up storage, stop force-quitting, update everything, trim background work, and check the battery. Do those in order and most old iPhones feel meaningfully faster for free.
