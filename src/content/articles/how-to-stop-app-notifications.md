# How to Stop Annoying App Notifications (Android and iPhone)

Notification spam is a modern problem with a quick fix. The apps you barely use are fighting for your attention dozens of times a day, and the solution is not to disable notifications globally — it's to take control app by app. Ten minutes of settings work, and your phone goes quiet while still telling you what actually matters.

Here's how to stop notification spam on both Android and iPhone, from the fast per-app fix to the power-user tools that schedule your quiet time.

## The fast fix: turn off notifications per app

The fastest win is silencing the worst offenders. You don't need to delete the app or wade through its in-app settings — the phone itself controls notifications.

**On Android:**

1. Open **Settings → Apps** (on some phones it's **Settings → Notifications → App settings**).
2. Scroll to the app that's annoying you.
3. Tap **Notifications**.
4. Turn off **All app notifications**, or use **Categories** (more on that below).

**On iPhone:**

1. Open **Settings → Notifications**.
2. Tap the app.
3. Toggle off **Allow Notifications**.

That's it. The app keeps working normally — you just stop hearing from it. If you're still getting pings from an app you turned off, force-quit it and reopen once; some apps hold stale notification requests.

## Use notification categories instead of a full block

Both platforms group an app's notifications into types, which lets you keep the useful ones and kill the noise. This is the underused power move of notification settings.

**On Android:** When you tap **Notifications** for an app, you'll see a list like "Messages," "Promotions," "Alerts," and "News." Toggle off just the ones that bother you. A shopping app can keep delivery updates while losing its daily sale blasts.

**On iPhone:** In the app's notification settings, you'll often see **Notification Groups** or app-specific toggles. Not every app exposes them, but many do — look for options like "Alerts," "Sound," and "Badges." You can keep badges off but banners on, or vice versa.

## Mute instead of block

Muting is the middle ground that most people never find. A muted notification still appears in your notification center, but it doesn't light up the screen, play a sound, or buzz.

- **On Android:** in the app's notification settings, choose **Silent** instead of a sound. Many apps also let you swipe a notification sideways to reveal a gear icon, which jumps straight to that app's settings.
- **On iPhone:** in the app's notification settings, turn off **Sounds** and **Banners** but leave the app in the list. You can also swipe left on any notification in the lock screen, tap **Options**, and choose **Deliver Quietly**.

## Set a schedule with Do Not Disturb and Focus modes

The per-app work solves the general noise. For the times you want everything quiet at once — sleep, meetings, dinner — both platforms have scheduled quiet modes.

**On Android:** open **Settings → Sound & vibration → Do Not Disturb** (the path varies by brand). You can set a schedule, decide who can still get through (favorites, contacts, repeated callers), and which apps are allowed to notify you during quiet hours. Focus mode is a separate, stronger version that can hide apps entirely for a set time.

**On iPhone:** open **Settings → Focus → Do Not Disturb**. Set a schedule (for example, 11 PM to 7 AM), choose who can interrupt you, and pick which apps are allowed. iOS Focus modes can also be tied to location — set one for "work" that goes quiet when you arrive at the office.

Both approaches share one idea: instead of turning notifications off and missing everything, you define the hours when notifications are allowed.

## Decide how each notification arrives

Beyond on/off, every notification has a "how loud is it" knob, and most people never touch it. A notification can arrive three ways:

- **Banner** — pops up on screen while you're using the phone.
- **Sound** — makes noise when it arrives.
- **Badge** — a number on the app icon waiting for you.

On iPhone, each of these has its own toggle under **Settings → Notifications → [app]**, so you can keep badges (the silent red number) while killing banners and sounds. That's the ideal setup for apps you want to acknowledge later but not be interrupted by. On Android, the equivalent lives in the app's notification categories, where types like "Alerts" and "Silent" can be set independently. Most people only need to do this for a handful of apps — the ten that actually matter to them.

## A small hygiene habit that pays off

The reason notification spam builds up is that new apps default to "notify me about everything." Get in the habit of handling it at install time: when an app asks for notification permission, you can tap **Don't Allow** or **Not Now** right then, instead of uninstalling and reinstalling the app later. Some apps will still nag; most respect the first refusal.

It's also worth doing a periodic sweep. Every few months, open the notification settings and ask yourself which apps you've actually welcomed a notification from in the past week. The list is almost always shorter than the list of apps currently pinging you. Silencing a few dormant apps at a time keeps the noise from creeping back, and the whole sweep takes about five minutes.

And if your phone feels sluggish beyond the noise, the constant notification churn is rarely the cause of slowness — but background app activity can be. ## Wrapping up

Killing notification spam is a three-step process: silence the worst apps one by one, use notification categories to keep only what's useful, and set a scheduled quiet mode for the hours you don't want to hear from anyone. Do that and your phone goes back to being a tool instead of a heckler.
