Starting a blog in 2026 is simpler than it's ever been — and harder to do *well*. This guide covers the practical setup step by step, then the part that actually determines success: publishing content people can find and trust.

## 1. Choose a niche you can sustain

A niche is the topic your blog owns. The best niche is one where:

- You know more than the average person, or you're willing to learn deeply.
- There's a real audience actively searching for answers.
- You can produce content for years without running out of ideas.

Narrow beats broad. "Windows troubleshooting for non-technical home users" is a better niche than "technology." Specific readers are easier to help, and easier to find.

## 2. Pick a name and a domain

- Keep it short, pronounceable, and memorable.
- Avoid numbers and hyphens.
- Check the domain is available (e.g. on Hostinger, Namecheap, or Google Domains alternatives).
- A `.com` is still the safest default, but `.net` or `.org` are fine for most purposes.

Your domain is your permanent address — don't overthink it, but don't rush it either.

## 3. Set up hosting

For a new blog, **shared hosting is all you need**. It's cheap, requires no technical skill, and handles the traffic a new blog gets.

- Recommended: Hostinger, SiteGround, or Bluehost shared plans ($2–$5/month for the first year).
- These come with one-click WordPress or a Git-based static deploy (like this very site uses).
- You don't need a VPS, dedicated server, or anything complicated at this stage.

## 4. Choose your platform

Your options, honestly compared:

| Platform | Best for | Trade-offs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **WordPress** (self-hosted) | Most people | Huge ecosystem, but plugins/updates need maintaining |
| **Static site generator** (Astro, Hugo, Eleventy) | Fast, secure, minimal | Slightly more setup; great performance and SEO |
| **WordPress.com / Medium / Substack** | Zero-hassle | Less control, weaker SEO, platform owns your audience |
| **Squarespace / Wix** | Design-first | Simpler but harder to rank and slower |

For a serious long-term blog, self-hosted WordPress or a static generator are the two sensible choices. This website runs on Astro — a static generator — because it's fast, secure, and runs anywhere.

## 5. Install your blog and pick a theme

- WordPress: most hosts have a one-click installer, then choose a lightweight theme.
- Static: clone your repository and run the build (the deployment guide in your repo's docs folder covers the exact steps).
- Choose a **fast, mobile-first theme**. A slow theme hurts readers *and* your search rankings.

## 6. Set up the essentials

Before writing, configure the basics:

- **Analytics** (Google Analytics or similar) — you need data to learn what works.
- **Search Console** — submit your sitemap so Google can index you properly.
- **Security**: keep everything updated, use strong passwords and two-factor login. A [password manager](/articles/password-manager-guide) makes the strong-password part painless.
- **The boring pages**: About, Contact, Privacy Policy, Terms, Disclaimer.

## 7. Publish your first real article

Not a "Hello world" post — an article that genuinely helps someone. Use the framework that works:

- **Target one search query** ("how to fix a slow Android phone").
- **Write a specific, honest title.**
- **Answer the question completely** with clear steps, examples, and structure.
- **Add internal links** to your other content once you have some.

One strong article beats five weak ones. Every article should be something you'd be proud to show a reader.

## 8. Use AI the right way

AI is a brilliant co-writer — this site uses it for drafting and research. The workflow that works:

1. Ask AI to research the topic and produce an outline and draft.
2. Add your own experience, examples, and corrections.
3. Fact-check everything that matters.
4. Edit for clarity and tone until it sounds like *you*.
5. Only then publish.

Never publish AI output unedited. Google's guidance explicitly rewards helpful, original content and penalizes unedited mass-produced material.

## 9. Get found on Google

- **Submit your sitemap** in Google Search Console.
- **Be patient and consistent.** Real organic traffic takes 3–6 months of steady publishing.
- **Write for readers first.** Keywords help, but thin content written only for search engines won't rank — and may get penalized.
- **Promote honestly**: share on relevant communities, answer questions where your expertise helps, build genuine backlinks.

## 10. Grow deliberately

After your first few months:

- **Double down on what works** — check Search Console and analytics for posts getting traffic, then write more in those areas.
- **Update old posts** to keep them fresh; updated evergreen content ranks well.
- **Build an email list** from day one (even a simple form) — search traffic is rented, your list is owned.
